## CHARACTER FUNCTION
Brennan is the audience's primary window into the investigation against Lucky Luciano. He provides the moral center of
the film -- a man with a deeply personal stake (his brother's heroin overdose death) who believes he is dismantling a
drug empire, only to discover he was unwittingly used as a pawn by a more ruthless criminal (Genovese) and his own
compromised director (Hartley). His arc embodies the film's central theme of Pyrrhic victory and unintended consequences.

## PERSONALITY TRAITS
- Driven by personal vengeance -- brother's death from heroin gives him an emotional stake beyond professional duty
- Believes in the mission and trusts institutional authority (initially trusts Director Hartley without question)
- Resourceful undercover operative -- rapidly recruits informants, documents intelligence, works under pressure
- Perceptive but not paranoid -- notices that Washington already seems to know his intelligence before he reports it, but
files it away rather than acting on suspicion
- Idealistic about law enforcement's role, which makes the final betrayal devastating
- Capable of forming unlikely alliances (with Delgado) across national and cultural lines

## RELATIONSHIPS
- **Director Hartley**: Brennan's handler and boss in Washington. Brennan trusts Hartley as the voice of authority and
mission clarity. Communicates via phone calls and telegrams. Hartley manipulates this trust, using Brennan's evidence to
build a case that was actually engineered by Genovese. Brennan ultimately confronts Hartley when the truth is revealed.
- **Carlos Delgado**: Forms an unlikely alliance with the Cuban Secret Police agent. They meet at the Hotel Nacional bar,
and Delgado eventually agrees to share information. Delgado warns Brennan they may be creating a bigger monster by
targeting Lucky instead of Genovese -- a warning Brennan ignores until too late.
- **Lucky Luciano**: The man Brennan holds personally responsible for his brother's death. Brennan's target throughout
the film. They never form a direct relationship -- Brennan observes and documents Lucky from a distance.
- **Meyer Lansky**: Possible source of the final revelation -- Lansky may be the one who tells Brennan the truth about
Genovese feeding intel to the FBN.

## CHARACTER ARC
- **Beginning State (Act 1)**: Watches Lucky's deportation from New York, uneasy that it won't end anything. Sent to
Havana by Hartley when intelligence reveals Lucky is in Cuba. Motivated by personal loss and institutional duty.
- **Inciting Incident**: Arrives in Havana and begins undercover work at the Hotel Nacional during the Havana Conference.
Recruits informants and witnesses the full scope of the mob summit.
- **Escalation (Act 2)**: Builds the case against Lucky with evidence from informants and Delgado. Reports to Hartley,
who praises him. Notices anomalies (Washington knowing things before he reports them) but trusts the system. Ignores
Delgado's warnings about Genovese being more dangerous.
- **Crisis Point**: Discovers the devastating truth -- Genovese has been feeding intelligence to the FBN the entire time,
Hartley knew, and the entire deportation was engineered by a rival mobster using the U.S. government as his weapon.
Confronts Hartley: "We didn't take down Lucky. Genovese did. We were his muscle."
- **End State**: Writes a report warning about Genovese and Hartley's complicity, but it will be buried. Watches Lucky
depart on the S.S. Bakir with doubt in his eyes. Realizes he was not the hunter but the weapon. Disillusionment arc --
loses faith in the institutions he served.
