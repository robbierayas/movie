## CHARACTER FUNCTION
Genovese is the main antagonist -- the greater evil that the "heroes" unknowingly empower. He is the film's embodiment of
the theme "the devil you know vs. the devil you don't." Where Lucky has restraint and a code, Genovese has none. He
represents ruthless, unchecked ambition: willing to use the United States government as his personal weapon, feed
intelligence to the FBN, and engineer a rival's deportation. His ultimate triumph is the film's ultimate tragedy -- the
heroin epidemic of the 1950s-60s is his legacy.

## PERSONALITY TRAITS
- Ambitious and calculating -- his every move is strategic, aimed at seizing Lucky's throne
- More ruthless and violent than Lucky -- no restraints, no code, no limits
- Masterful manipulator -- feeds intelligence to the FBN to weaponize the U.S. government against his rival, a level of
cunning that goes beyond brute force
- Patient when necessary -- challenges Lucky at the Conference, loses, but doesn't abandon his plan; pivots to the long
game of feeding the FBN
- Gloating in victory -- visits Lucky at Tiscornia to reveal what he's done, demands the heroin network
- Willing to assassinate allies -- orders Anastasia's murder in 1957 for the crime of loyalty to Lucky
- Aggressive expansionist -- plans to build the heroin network bigger and more aggressively than Lucky ever would, with
no restraint

## RELATIONSHIPS
- **Lucky Luciano**: Rival and target. Genovese is Lucky's underboss who wants the throne. Challenges Lucky openly at the
Havana Conference and loses. Secretly feeds intel to the FBN to engineer Lucky's deportation. Visits Lucky at Tiscornia
to gloat and demand control of the heroin network. Lucky warns: "You'll destroy everything." Genovese: "I'll build
something better."
- **Albert Anastasia**: Enemy. Anastasia's fierce loyalty to Lucky and his famous defense at the Conference make him a
direct obstacle to Genovese's ambitions. Genovese will order Anastasia's assassination in 1957 -- the ultimate revenge
for that moment of defiance.
- **Director Hartley/FBN**: Genovese's unwitting weapon. He feeds the FBN intelligence on Lucky's operations, knowing
Hartley will use it for political gain. The FBN becomes his personal hit squad. Genovese played the federal government to
take Lucky's throne.
- **Frank Costello**: Another of Lucky's allies who Genovese will force out in 1957, consolidating his power.
- **Meyer Lansky**: Lucky's partner and a target. After Lucky's deportation, Genovese moves on everything they built.
Lansky sees it coming.

## CHARACTER ARC
- **Beginning State (Act 2)**: Present at the Havana Conference as Lucky's underboss. Subtly pushes back on Lucky's
authority in the Inner Circle meeting, testing the waters.
- **Challenge**: Openly challenges Lucky's authority from exile at the full Conference session. Loses when Anastasia
defends Lucky and the vote goes against him. Forced into a humiliating handshake.
- **Pivot**: Rather than accept defeat, begins making secret phone calls -- feeding intelligence to the FBN. Uses the
U.S. government as his weapon to accomplish what he couldn't do in the Conference room.
- **Victory (Act 3)**: Visits Lucky at Tiscornia to gloat. Reveals everything: "I fed them everything they needed."
Demands the heroin network. Reveals his expansion plans -- bigger, more aggressive, no restraint.
- **Legacy (Epilogue)**: Takes over. French Connection fully operational. Over 80% of U.S. heroin from his network.
Orders Anastasia assassinated (1957). Forces out Costello (1957). Floods American cities with heroin. The 1950s-60s
epidemic is his doing. Finally arrested for narcotics in 1959. Dies in federal prison 1969.
- **Arc Type**: Negative triumph -- Genovese wins everything he wanted, and the cost is borne by millions.
