# DIRECTOR WILLIAM HARTLEY

## BASIC INFORMATION

- **Family**: Not mentioned
- **Historical/Composite**: Composite character based on Harry Anslinger (FBN Director 1930-1962), who historically used

## CHARACTER FUNCTION
Hartley represents institutional complicity and the corruption that enables the film's tragic outcome. He is the man who
knowingly accepts intelligence from a mobster (Genovese) staging a coup against a rival (Lucky), using the FBN as
Genovese's weapon because it serves his own political ambitions. He manipulates Brennan, his loyal agent, and engineers
the ultimatum that forces Cuba to deport Lucky. Hartley embodies the theme that sometimes the system itself is corrupt,
and "doing the right thing" within a corrupt system produces worse outcomes.

## PERSONALITY TRAITS
- Cold, pragmatic, and politically ambitious -- prioritizes career-making cases over justice
- Manipulative handler -- sends Brennan to Havana knowing the personal stakes will keep him motivated, praises Brennan's
work while already possessing the same intelligence from Genovese
- Morally compromised -- knowingly accepts intelligence from a mob underboss engineering a power grab, doesn't care about
the consequences as long as he gets the political win
- Unapologetic when confronted -- "Luciano is gone. That's what matters."
- Skilled at leveraging political pressure -- proposes the economic blackmail of Cuba to the State Department, uses the
Sinatra photos scandal to pressure action
- Never appears in Havana -- operates entirely from Washington D.C. via phone calls and telegrams, making him a remote,
controlling presence

## RELATIONSHIPS
- **Jack Brennan**: His field agent and unwitting instrument. Hartley exploits Brennan's personal motivation (brother's
death) and professional trust. Serves as Brennan's handler via phone/telegram -- the voice of authority and mission
clarity. In reality, uses Brennan to confirm intelligence he already has from Genovese, making the case airtight.
- **Vito Genovese**: His secret intelligence source. Genovese feeds Hartley information on Lucky's operations, and
Hartley takes it knowing the source is a rival mobster trying to seize power. This relationship is the hidden engine
driving the entire plot.
- **Lucky Luciano**: His target -- the political trophy. Hartley wants the career-making case of deporting America's most
famous mob boss.
- **U.S. State Department**: Hartley pressures them into the medical supply ultimatum against Cuba, leveraging Brennan's
evidence combined with Genovese's intel.

## CHARACTER ARC
- **Beginning State (Act 1)**: Present at Lucky's deportation from New York, satisfied the case is closed. When
intelligence reveals Lucky is in Cuba, brings Brennan in for the assignment, knowing it's personal for him.
- **Escalation (Act 2)**: Operates as Brennan's handler from Washington. Receives reports, gives tactical guidance, asks
probing questions (already knowing answers from Genovese). Deflects when Brennan reports on Genovese's suspicious
behavior: "Stay focused on Luciano." Uses Sinatra photos to pressure the State Department. Proposes the economic
blackmail ultimatum.
- **End State (Act 3)**: Tells Brennan "Your evidence made this possible" when the ultimatum works. When confronted with
the truth, is unapologetic: "And the result is the same." Represents the system's indifference to consequences -- the
mission is accomplished regardless of who benefits.
- **Arc Type**: Flat/Negative -- Hartley doesn't change because he was never operating in good faith. He is what he
always was: a pragmatist who uses whatever tools are available, including mob informants and loyal agents, to achieve
political victories.
