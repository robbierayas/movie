# SIMILAR MOVIES & SHOWS - REFERENCE FOR DIFFERENTIATION

Purpose: Avoid copying elements from these works. Know what's been done so Lucky in Havana tells its own story.

---

## THE GODFATHER PART II (1974)
**Director:** Francis Ford Coppola
**Overlap:** Havana Conference setting, mob boss visiting Cuba, betrayal within the family, Cuban revolution backdrop.
- Michael Corleone attends a business conference in Havana (late 1950s, not 1946)
- Hyman Roth character (based on Meyer Lansky) -- the "Jewish mob financier" archetype
- Fredo's betrayal mirrors internal mob treachery
- New Year's Eve revolution as backdrop to mob business
- Cuba as a paradise about to be lost

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Set in 1946-47, a decade earlier -- Cuba is stable under Batista, not on the verge of revolution
- Lucky IS the boss, not a visiting heir trying to prove himself
- The betrayal comes from OUTSIDE the system (Genovese using the FBN), not from within the family
- Pyrrhic victory theme -- the "heroes" make things worse, not the villains
- FBN agent and Cuban cop as co-protagonists, not just mob perspective
- The conference is the real Havana Conference (historical), not a fictional summit

---

## BUGSY (1991)
**Director:** Barry Levinson
**Overlap:** Bugsy Siegel / Flamingo Hotel, Lansky relationship, mob financing, Virginia Hill.
- Siegel's Las Vegas vision and the Flamingo Hotel disaster
- Lansky as the financial partner trying to rein Siegel in
- The Commission debating whether to kill Siegel over cost overruns
- Glamour of mob lifestyle in the 1940s

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Bugsy Siegel is a subplot (Scenes 13-14), not the main story
- Focus is on the DECISION about Siegel, not Siegel himself -- Lucky using his authority to grant a reprieve
- The Bugsy situation illustrates Lucky's code: he bends the rules through legitimate channels, not by breaking them
- Cuba, not Las Vegas, is the setting

---

## BOARDWALK EMPIRE (TV, 2010-2014)
**Creator:** Terence Winter
**Overlap:** Lucky Luciano as recurring character, rise through organized crime, Lansky, Anastasia, mob politics, period setting.
- Depicts young Lucky's rise from street criminal to boss
- Shows Lansky, Anastasia, and other historical figures
- Prohibition-era organized crime, transitioning to post-Prohibition

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Lucky is at the END of his American power, not the beginning
- Set in 1946-47, post-war era, not Prohibition
- Focus on exile, not rise -- a king without a kingdom
- The moral center is Brennan and Delgado, not the mob figures
- Thematic focus on unintended consequences, not empire-building

---

## MOB CITY (TV, 2013)
**Creator:** Frank Darabont
**Overlap:** Bugsy Siegel in LA, organized crime in the 1940s, noir aesthetic.
- 1940s Los Angeles organized crime
- Bugsy Siegel as a major character
- Noir visual style and detective protagonist

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Havana setting, not LA
- Lucky Luciano, not Bugsy Siegel, as the central mob figure
- International scope (Cuba, Washington, New York) vs. single-city story
- The FBN agent is unknowingly being used, not a traditional detective narrative

---

## LANSKY (2021)
**Director:** Eytan Rockaway
**Overlap:** Meyer Lansky biopic, Cuba gambling operations, mob financial empire.
- Lansky's life story told in flashback
- His Cuba casino operations
- Relationship with Lucky and other mob figures

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Lansky is a supporting character, not the protagonist
- Focus on the specific five-month period in Cuba, not a lifetime biopic
- The FBN/government manipulation angle is absent from Lansky's story
- Delgado's Cuban perspective provides a viewpoint these films never explore

---

## HAVANA (1990)
**Director:** Sydney Pollack
**Overlap:** 1950s Cuba setting, American in Havana, political upheaval.
- Robert Redford as an American gambler in pre-revolution Havana
- Romance and political intrigue in Cuba
- The end of an era in Havana

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Set a full decade earlier (1946-47 vs. late 1950s)
- No revolution backdrop -- Cuba is stable, corrupt, and profitable
- Based on real historical events, not fiction
- Crime/thriller, not romantic drama

---

## THE LOST CITY (2005)
**Director:** Andy Garcia
**Overlap:** Cuban revolution, Havana setting, loss of a way of life.
- A Cuban family's experience during the revolution
- Havana as paradise lost
- Political upheaval destroying personal lives

**How Lucky in Havana is different:**
- Pre-revolution era -- Cuba is the mob's playground, not yet in political crisis
- American and mob perspectives, not Cuban family drama
- The "loss" is Lucky's personal exile, not a national upheaval

## THE HYMAN ROTH CONNECTION
**Note:** The Godfather Part II's Hyman Roth (Lee Strasberg) is directly based on Meyer Lansky.
- Roth is depicted as a quiet, calculating businessman -- "I'm a retired investor on a pension"
- His Cuba operations mirror Lansky's real investments
- His manipulation of Michael mirrors mob political gamesmanship

**Differentiation for our Lansky:**
- Show Lansky as Lucky's genuine friend and partner, not a manipulator
- Emphasize Lansky's loyalty -- he warns Lucky about Genovese, visits him at Tiscornia
- Lansky as the man who sees the future clearly but can't prevent it
- Avoid the "quiet old man" archetype -- our Lansky is 44, still active, still building

## WHAT MAKES LUCKY IN HAVANA UNIQUE

Elements that NO similar film has explored:

1. **Pyrrhic Victory Theme**: The "heroes" (Brennan, Delgado) WIN -- and that victory is the tragedy. No similar film makes the law enforcement victory the source of the catastrophe.

2. **FBN as Mob Weapon**: Genovese using a federal agency to eliminate his rival. The government doesn't just fail -- it's actively weaponized by the villain. This structural irony is unique.

3. **Delgado's Perspective**: A Cuban cop caught between American power and mob money. No Havana-set film has explored this viewpoint -- the local man who sees everything, understands everyone, and can't stop any of it.

4. **The Code (Omerta) as Tragic Virtue**: Lucky's adherence to the Commission system is what makes him trustworthy and what makes his removal catastrophic. His code is his strength AND his vulnerability -- Genovese breaks the code to win.

5. **The Medical Supply Ultimatum**: The specific historical mechanism of Lucky's deportation -- threatening Cuba's hospitals -- has never been dramatized. It's a uniquely cruel form of leverage that implicates the U.S. government.

6. **Dual Protagonist Structure**: Brennan (outsider looking in) and Delgado (insider looking out) provide complementary perspectives that no single-viewpoint mob film can achieve.
