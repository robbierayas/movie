# Scene 27 - Epilogue Montage (MONTAGE)

## TYPE
Linear Montage / Historical Montage

## PURPOSE
Compress three decades of consequences stemming from Lucky's deportation. Show the audience that the "victory" of
removing Lucky from Cuba directly led to a cascade of violence, a heroin epidemic, assassinations, and the rise of a
worse criminal empire -- everything Brennan and Delgado feared. The Pyrrhic victory theme made concrete through
historical outcomes.

## TIME SPAN
April 1947 - Early 1970s (approximately 25 years)

## MONTAGE STRUCTURE

### Shot 1 - Italy Arrival (April 11, 1947)
The S.S. Bakir arrives in Genoa harbor. Lucky is arrested by Italian authorities on the dock. Detained for 10 days, then
released. He settles in Naples -- a man with an empire but no country to run it from.

### Shot 2 - French Connection Operational (1951)
The heroin pipeline Lucky and the Corsicans built goes fully operational: Turkey to Sicily to France to Cuba to U.S.
ports. The network wasn't destroyed by Lucky's deportation -- it was inherited. Over 80% of U.S. heroin will flow from
this route.

### Shot 3 - Anastasia Assassinated (October 25, 1957)
Albert Anastasia -- the man who stood up at the Havana Conference and declared "For me, you are the Big Boss" -- is
gunned down in a barbershop chair. Genovese's orders. The price of loyalty to Lucky, paid ten years later.

### Shot 4 - Costello Forced Out (1957)
Frank Costello, Lucky's ally and acting boss, is forced from power. Genovese consolidates control. The old guard that
Lucky built is being systematically dismantled.

### Shot 5 - Heroin Epidemic (1950s-1960s)
American cities flooded with heroin. The epidemic Brennan's brother died from doesn't end -- it gets catastrophically
worse. Genovese's aggressive expansion, with no restraint, no code, delivers exactly what Delgado warned about: a bigger
monster.

### Shot 6 - Genovese Arrested (1959)
Genovese is finally arrested for narcotics. But by then the damage is done -- the networks he built will outlive him. The
empire runs itself.

### Shot 7 - Castro's Revolution (January 1, 1959)
Castro enters Havana. The mob is expelled from Cuba. Everything Valdés built, everything Lansky invested, everything
Lucky envisioned -- gone. The paradise is over. Lansky loses everything.

### Shot 8 - Lucky Dies (January 26, 1962)
Lucky Luciano dies of a heart attack in Naples, age 64. He never returned to Cuba. He never returned to America. The
exile king dies in exile.

### Shot 9 - Genovese Dies (1969)
Genovese dies in federal prison. The man who used the U.S. government as his weapon ends up in a government cage. But his
heroin empire outlives him.

### Shot 10 - French Connection Broken (Early 1970s)
The French Connection is finally broken -- 25 years after Lucky and the Corsicans first built it. A quarter century of
heroin, hundreds of thousands of lives destroyed. The network Brennan thought he was dismantling in 1947 wasn't stopped
until the 1970s.

## FULL SCRIPT

MONTAGE - THE CONSEQUENCES

FADE IN:

TITLE CARD: "APRIL 11, 1947 -- GENOA, ITALY"

EXT. GENOA HARBOR - DAY

The S.S. Bakir limps into port. Twenty-three days at sea. LUCKY LUCIANO descends the gangway
into a crowd of ITALIAN POLICE and PRESS PHOTOGRAPHERS. He is arrested on the dock. Flashbulbs
pop. He is led to a police car, calm, composed, a man for whom this has become routine.

TITLE CARD: "HE WAS DETAINED FOR TEN DAYS, THEN RELEASED. HE SETTLED IN NAPLES."

EXT. NAPLES STREET - DAY

Lucky walks alone through a Naples street. An old man in an overcoat, collar up, passing cafes
and storefronts. A man with an empire and no country to run it from.

TITLE CARD: "1951 -- THE FRENCH CONNECTION GOES FULLY OPERATIONAL"

EXT. MARSEILLE DOCKS - DAY (1951)

Crates loaded onto a cargo ship. MEN in work clothes move with purpose. Coded labels. A
pipeline from Turkey through Sicily through France to American ports. The network Lucky built
doesn't die with his deportation. It thrives.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          The heroin network didn't stop when
          Lucky left Cuba. It expanded. Over eighty
          percent of the heroin entering the United
          States would flow through the French
          Connection for the next two decades.

TITLE CARD: "OCTOBER 25, 1957"

INT. BARBERSHOP - DAY (1957)

A barbershop. White tile. Chrome chairs. ALBERT ANASTASIA sits in the chair, a hot towel over
his face. Relaxed. Unaware.

TWO MEN enter. They move fast. Guns out.

The towel falls. Anastasia's eyes go wide.

GUNFIRE. The mirrors shatter. Anastasia falls from the chair.

TITLE CARD: "ALBERT ANASTASIA -- THE MAN WHO STOOD UP FOR LUCKY -- ASSASSINATED ON
GENOVESE'S ORDERS."

INT. RESTAURANT - NIGHT (1957)

FRANK COSTELLO sits alone at a table. A WAITER hands him a note. Costello reads it, sets it
down. He understands. He pushes back from the table and walks out. He doesn't come back.

TITLE CARD: "1957 -- FRANK COSTELLO FORCED FROM POWER. GENOVESE CONSOLIDATES CONTROL."

TITLE CARD: "1950s-1960s -- THE EPIDEMIC"

MONTAGE - AMERICAN CITIES:

- A syringe on a tenement floor. A YOUNG MAN slumped against a wall.
- Hospital corridors. DOCTORS moving between beds. Overdose after overdose.
- Newspaper headlines stacking: "HEROIN CRISIS GRIPS CITY" -- "NARCOTICS DEATHS DOUBLE" --
  "EPIDEMIC OF THE NEEDLE"
- Street corners. DEALERS and ADDICTS. The human cost of the pipeline, measured in bodies.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Genovese built it bigger. No restraint, no
          code, no limits. Exactly what Delgado warned
          me. Exactly what I didn't listen to.

TITLE CARD: "1959 -- GENOVESE ARRESTED FOR NARCOTICS CONSPIRACY"

INT. COURTROOM - DAY (1959)

VITO GENOVESE stands in a courtroom. Handcuffed. The judge's voice, muffled. Genovese stares
straight ahead, his face empty. The empire runs itself now. His arrest changes nothing.

TITLE CARD: "JANUARY 1, 1959 -- HAVANA"

EXT. HAVANA STREETS - DAY (1959)

Revolution. CROWDS flood the streets. Cuban flags wave. SOLDIERS ride on trucks. The casinos
are shuttered. The Hotel Nacional stands silent.

CARLOS DELGADO stands on a Havana sidewalk, watching the revolution pass. MARIA beside him,
holding the hands of ELENA and SOFIA, older now -- teenagers. The world Delgado served has
been swept away.

TITLE CARD: "CASTRO'S REVOLUTION. THE MOB EXPELLED FROM CUBA. EVERYTHING VALDES BUILT --
GONE. EVERYTHING LANSKY INVESTED -- GONE."

EXT. NAPLES STREET - DAY (1962)

An older Lucky Luciano walks through a Naples park. Alone. Gray hair, slower step. He sits on
a bench. Reaches into his pocket for a cigarette. His hand trembles.

He clutches his chest. The cigarette falls.

TITLE CARD: "JANUARY 26, 1962 -- LUCKY LUCIANO DIES OF A HEART ATTACK IN NAPLES. AGE 64.
HE NEVER RETURNED TO CUBA. HE NEVER RETURNED TO AMERICA."

INT. PRISON CELL - DAY (1969)

A small cell. A cot. A window with bars. VITO GENOVESE lies on the cot, staring at the
ceiling. He is old, gaunt, diminished. The empire builder, caged.

TITLE CARD: "1969 -- VITO GENOVESE DIES IN FEDERAL PRISON. THE MAN WHO USED THE U.S.
GOVERNMENT AS HIS WEAPON ENDS UP IN A GOVERNMENT CAGE."

TITLE CARD: "EARLY 1970s"

EXT. MARSEILLE DOCKS - DAY (1970s)

Police raids. AGENTS in windbreakers storm warehouses. Crates broken open. White powder spills
across concrete floors.

TITLE CARD: "THE FRENCH CONNECTION IS FINALLY BROKEN -- TWENTY-FIVE YEARS AFTER LUCKY AND
THE CORSICANS BUILT IT. A QUARTER CENTURY OF HEROIN. HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS OF LIVES."

SMASH TO BLACK.

Silence.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          We won. That's what they told us.
          We deported Lucky Luciano and we won.

A beat.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.) (CONT'D)
          We didn't win anything.

## Production Notes
- Historical dates should be displayed on screen (title cards or supers)
- Consider newsreel/archival footage aesthetic for some shots
- Anastasia barbershop assassination is an iconic historical image
- The heroin epidemic shots should be visceral -- this is the consequence of the "victory"
- Castro's revolution ties directly to Valdés and Lansky's Cuba storylines
- Final shots (Lucky's death, Genovese's death) should feel like the closing of a ledger
- Music: consider period-appropriate transitions as decades pass
