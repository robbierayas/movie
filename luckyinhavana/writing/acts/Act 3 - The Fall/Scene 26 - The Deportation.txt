# Scene 26 - The Deportation

## LOCATION
EXT. - HAVANA HARBOR - NIGHT (March 19, 1947)

## CHARACTERS
Lucky Luciano, Igea Lissoni, Jack Brennan, Carlos Delgado, Maria Delgado, Delgado's Daughters

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
Late evening, March 19, 1947. Havana Harbor. The S.S. Bakir -- a rusty Turkish cargo freighter, a far cry from the S.S.
Laura Keene that brought Lucky to exile in Scene 1. Lucky's 141 days in Cuba end not with a grand departure but with a
humiliating passage on a cargo ship. The harbor is dark, the mood heavy.

### Key Beats
1. Igea's tearful goodbye. The woman who accompanied Lucky through Cuba, who was with him at El Jardin when he was
arrested, now says goodbye on a dock. The personal cost of the political operation is on full display.
2. Lucky boards the S.S. Bakir for the 23-day voyage to Italy. His final words carry historical weight -- the quote:
"I'll be back when the current government is gone." He never returned. He died in Naples in 1962. Castro took power in
1959. The irony: Lucky was right about the government changing, but he was wrong about coming back.
3. Brennan watches from the dock. This should have been his victory moment -- the mission accomplished, the man deported.
Instead, doubt fills his eyes. He knows now what he helped set in motion. Genovese used him, Hartley used him, and the
heroin network won't stop -- it will expand. Separately, Delgado returns home to his family, holds his daughters,
troubled by everything.

### Character Moments
- **Lucky Luciano**: Departs Cuba with the same composure he arrived with, but stripped of everything -- the mansion, the
empire, the freedom. His historical quote is both defiant and tragically wrong. The exile king exiled again.
- **Igea Lissoni**: The emotional heart of the deportation scene. Her goodbye humanizes what is otherwise a political and
criminal operation.
- **Jack Brennan**: Watches from the dock with the knowledge that his "victory" empowered Genovese. The doubt in his eyes
is the culmination of his entire arc -- from righteous avenger to unwitting pawn.
- **Carlos Delgado**: Returns home to Maria and his daughters. Holds them close. Troubled. The assignment is over, but
the cost is still being counted.

### Closing
The S.S. Bakir pulls away from Havana Harbor into the dark water. Lucky sailing toward Italy, toward exile, toward death
in 1962. Brennan on the dock, knowing what comes next. Delgado at home, holding his family. The story's three threads --
the mobster, the agent, the cop -- diverge for the last time before the epilogue.

## FULL SCRIPT

EXT. HAVANA HARBOR - NIGHT (MARCH 19, 1947)

SUPER: HAVANA HARBOR -- MARCH 19, 1947

Night. The harbor is dark except for dock lights casting yellow pools on the concrete pier.
The water is black, restless, slapping against the pilings.

The S.S. BAKIR sits at the dock -- a Turkish cargo freighter. Old, rust-streaked, the paint
peeling from her hull. She is nothing like the S.S. Laura Keene that carried Lucky out of
New York a year ago. She is a ship for cargo and deportees, not passengers and promises.

The gangway is down. Crew members move in the shadows, preparing for departure.

A police escort arrives. Two cars. The first carries LUCKY LUCIANO, flanked by OFFICERS. The
second carries his luggage -- two suitcases. Not much for a man who owned a mansion.

Lucky steps out of the car. He's in a dark overcoat, collar up against the harbor wind. He
looks at the ship. Takes it in. The rust, the size, the indignity of it.

He doesn't complain. He never complains.

IGEA LISSONI is waiting on the pier. She's been crying -- her eyes are red, her face drawn.
She wears a dark coat, clutching it around herself. When she sees Lucky, she moves toward him.

The officers step aside. Even they understand that this moment belongs to the two of them.

Igea reaches Lucky. She takes his hands.

                    IGEA
          Charlie.

                    LUCKY
          Hey.

                    IGEA
          I tried to get them to let me come
          with you. They said no.

                    LUCKY
          I know.

                    IGEA
          Twenty-three days on that ship. Alone.

                    LUCKY
          I've been alone before. I'll manage.

She presses her forehead against his chest. His arms go around her. He holds her -- tightly,
the way a man holds something he's losing.

                    IGEA
          When will I see you?

                    LUCKY
          Come to Naples. When things settle down,
          come to Naples. I'll be there.

                    IGEA
          Promise me.

                    LUCKY
          I promise.

She pulls back. Looks at his face. Touches it -- her palm against his cheek, memorizing the
feel of it.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          Don't cry on the dock. Not in front
          of them.

She nods. Wipes her eyes. Straightens.

                    IGEA
          I'm not crying.

                    LUCKY
          Good.

He kisses her. Brief. Firm. Then releases her.

AT THE EDGE OF THE PIER -- fifty yards away, in the shadows beyond the dock lights:

JACK BRENNAN leans against a warehouse wall. Watching. His hands are in his pockets. He's
alone. No telegram to send, no report to file. Just a man watching the result of his work.

This should be his victory. The mission accomplished. The man deported. Instead, Brennan
watches with the hollow eyes of a man who knows that what he's seeing is not an ending but
a beginning -- the beginning of something worse.

BACK ON THE PIER:

An OFFICER approaches Lucky.

                    OFFICER
          Senor Luciano. It is time.

Lucky nods. He picks up one of his suitcases. An officer takes the other.

He walks toward the gangway. Stops at the bottom. Turns and looks back at Havana.

The city spreads beyond the harbor -- lights twinkling in the hills of Vedado, the glow of the
Malecon, the distant silhouette of the Hotel Nacional on its bluff. The city that was his
kingdom for one hundred and forty-one days.

Lucky looks at Igea. She stands where he left her, arms wrapped around herself.

He looks at the city one more time.

                    LUCKY
          I'll be back when the current government
          is gone.

He turns and walks up the gangway. His shoes ring on the metal steps. The officers wait below.
The ship's crew takes his suitcase.

Lucky reaches the deck. He moves to the railing and looks down at the pier. At Igea. At the
officers. At the harbor and the city beyond.

A HORN sounds. Deep, low, echoing across the water. The crew begins to cast off lines. The
gangway is pulled away.

The S.S. Bakir shudders. The engines engage. Slowly -- agonizingly slowly -- the ship begins
to pull away from the pier.

Igea watches from the dock. She raises one hand. Lucky raises his.

The gap between the ship and the pier widens. Ten feet. Twenty. Fifty.

ON THE PIER:

Brennan watches the ship pull away. The doubt in his eyes has hardened into something else --
certainty. The certainty that he helped do the wrong thing for the right reasons, and the
consequences will be paid by people who had nothing to do with any of it.

He turns and walks away from the harbor. Into the dark streets of Havana. Toward the airport.
Toward Washington. Toward the report that no one will read.

INT. DELGADO HOME - LIVING ROOM - NIGHT

A small, warm home. Modest furniture. Family photographs on the wall. The sounds of a Havana
night drifting through open windows -- music, voices, the city that goes on living.

CARLOS DELGADO walks through the front door. He removes his jacket. Hangs it on a hook.

MARIA DELGADO emerges from the kitchen. She reads his face and says nothing. She's learned
not to ask about certain days.

Two YOUNG GIRLS -- ANA and SOFIA, Delgado's daughters -- run from the back room.

                    ANA
          Papa!

                    SOFIA
          Papa, you're home!

Delgado kneels. Catches them both. Pulls them into his arms and holds them -- fiercely, as if
the world outside might take them too.

                    DELGADO
          I'm home.

Maria watches from the doorway. She knows. Not the details -- never the details. But she knows
that something ended today and that her husband is carrying the weight of it.

Delgado lifts his daughters. One in each arm. Carries them toward the back of the house. Maria
follows.

The front door stays open. Through it, the sounds of Havana at night. The city doesn't know
that Lucky Luciano just sailed away on a cargo ship. The city doesn't know what comes next.
Nobody does.

EXT. HAVANA HARBOR - NIGHT

The S.S. Bakir moves through the harbor mouth into open water. Her lights grow smaller. The
rusty hull vanishes into the darkness of the Straits of Florida.

LUCKY stands at the stern railing. Havana's lights are a line on the horizon, shrinking.

Ninety miles that way is Florida. The country that exiled him. The country that followed him
to Cuba. The country that used its hospitals and its leverage and its Bureau to push him out
one more time.

The lights disappear. The ocean is black in every direction.

Lucky turns from the railing. Walks toward the hatch. Twenty-three days to Italy. To Naples.
To exile.

The exile king, exiled again.

FADE OUT.

## Production Notes
- Historical accuracy: March 19, 1947, late evening, S.S. Bakir (Turkish cargo freighter)
- Include the historical quote verbatim: "I'll be back when the current government is gone."
- Visual bookend with Scene 1 (The Deportation from New York) -- another ship, another exile
- The rusty cargo freighter contrasts with the luxury of Lucky's Cuba life
- Three-way cross-cutting at the end: Lucky on the ship, Brennan on the dock, Delgado at home
- 23-day voyage to Italy (connects to epilogue montage opening)
