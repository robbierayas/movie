# Scene 25 - Delgado's Goodbye

## LOCATION
EXT. - TISCORNIA IMMIGRATION CAMP - DAY (Mid-March 1947)

## CHARACTERS
Lucky Luciano, Carlos Delgado

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
Delgado arrives at Tiscornia one last time before Lucky's deportation. This is the third visit to the detention camp
fence -- after Lansky's farewell and Genovese's gloating. This one is different: quiet, personal, between two men who
spent months together and developed a complicated mutual respect.

### Key Beats
1. Delgado and Lucky face each other at the fence. The dynamic has completely shifted from Scene 5 (The New Home) where
Delgado was the security detail and Lucky was the charming boss. Now Lucky is detained and Delgado is the man who helped
put him there. Yet there's no animosity.
2. Mutual respect despite being on opposite sides. Delgado protected Lucky, watched over him, learned his routines, saw
the man behind the mobster. He also provided the schedule that led to Lucky's arrest. Lucky understands -- Delgado was
doing his job, the only honest man in the whole arrangement.
3. Lucky doesn't hold it against Delgado. In a world of corrupt officials, compromised agents, and scheming mobsters,
Delgado was straightforward. He served his country even when his country didn't deserve it. Lucky can respect that, even
from behind a fence.

### Character Moments
- **Carlos Delgado**: Visits the man he helped bring down. The weight of the entire story sits on him -- assigned to
protect Lucky, he came to understand him, and then he betrayed that trust for what he believed was right. There's no
triumph in this visit, only the quiet acknowledgment of what they both went through.
- **Lucky Luciano**: Shows the side of him that earned Delgado's respect in the first place. Gracious in defeat,
understanding of Delgado's position. In forgiving Delgado, Lucky demonstrates the restraint and code that distinguished
him from Genovese -- the very qualities that make his removal tragic.

### Closing
Delgado leaves Tiscornia for the last time. The relationship that began as a professional assignment -- protect this man,
keep him comfortable -- ends as something more complicated and human. Two men who understood each other, on opposite
sides, saying goodbye.

## FULL SCRIPT

EXT. TISCORNIA IMMIGRATION CAMP - LATE AFTERNOON (MID-MARCH 1947)

SUPER: TISCORNIA DETENTION CAMP -- MID-MARCH 1947

Late afternoon. The sun is lower now, the light softer than during the previous visits.
The compound looks almost gentle in the golden hour -- the concrete less harsh, the fence
less sharp. Almost.

CARLOS DELGADO walks toward the visiting fence. He's in his dark suit, clean-shaven, the same
military bearing as the day he was assigned to Lucky's detail at the airport. But he moves
differently now -- slower, carrying something invisible.

He reaches the fence. Waits.

LUCKY LUCIANO emerges from the barracks. He walks to the fence. Sees Delgado.

A beat. The two men look at each other through the chain link. The bodyguard and the man he was
assigned to protect. The cop and the criminal. The betrayer and the forgiven.

                    LUCKY
          Carlos.

                    DELGADO
          Senor Luciano.

                    LUCKY
          Five months and you're still calling
          me that.

                    DELGADO
          It is your name.

                    LUCKY
          My name is Charlie. My friends call
          me Charlie.

A silence. Delgado absorbs the word. Friends. After everything.

                    DELGADO
          I came to --
               (stops)
          I don't know what I came to say.

                    LUCKY
          Then don't say anything. Just stand
          there. It's good to see a familiar face.

They stand in silence for a moment. The yard is mostly empty. A few other detainees in the
distance. A guard leaning against a wall, smoking.

                    DELGADO
          I gave them your schedule. Saturday lunch.
          El Jardin. One o'clock. The table near the
          garden.

                    LUCKY
          I know.

                    DELGADO
          You know?

                    LUCKY
          Carlos. You drove me every Saturday for
          five months. You knew my routine better
          than I did. When the arrest happened exactly
          where I always sit, at exactly the time I
          always arrive -- who else would it be?

Delgado's face tightens. He expected anger. Expected Lucky to look at him the way Genovese
would -- with vengeance, with calculation, with the cold arithmetic of betrayal.

Instead, Lucky shrugs.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          You did your job. That's more than I can
          say for most of the people I've dealt with
          on this island.

                    DELGADO
          It was not just my job.

                    LUCKY
          No?

                    DELGADO
          I believed it was the right thing.
          Brennan said -- the heroin, the network,
          the damage it would do. I believed him.
          I believed we were stopping something
          terrible.

                    LUCKY
          And now?

Delgado looks at the ground. Then back at Lucky.

                    DELGADO
          Now I am not sure. I told Brennan that
          removing you might create something worse.
          I told him about Genovese. I told him that
          the man who challenged you at the conference
          has no limits.

                    LUCKY
          You were right.

                    DELGADO
          I didn't want to be right.

Another silence. The sun moves lower. The shadows of the fence posts lengthen across the dirt
yard.

                    LUCKY
          You got a good family, Carlos. Your wife,
          your two girls. I remember you told me
          their names once. I forget now.

                    DELGADO
          Ana and Sofia.

                    LUCKY
          Ana and Sofia. Go home to Ana and
          Sofia. Forget about all of this.

                    DELGADO
          Can I forget?

                    LUCKY
          Probably not. But go home anyway.

Lucky steps closer to the fence. His voice drops. Not conspiratorial -- intimate. The voice of
one man offering another something honest.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          Carlos. You were the only honest man
          in this whole arrangement. Valdes took
          his envelopes. The police took their
          orders. The government took the Americans'
          money and then folded to the Americans'
          threats. Your FBN friend was being used
          by people he trusted.
               (beat)
          You were the only one who did what he
          did because he believed it was right.
          That counts for something. Even to a
          man like me.

Delgado's eyes are bright. He blinks. Looks away.

                    DELGADO
          You are not what I expected.

                    LUCKY
          What did you expect?

                    DELGADO
          A monster.

                    LUCKY
          Maybe I am one. But there are worse
          monsters. You know that now.

Delgado nods. Slowly.

                    DELGADO
          The ship leaves in a few days. The Bakir.
          A Turkish cargo freighter.

                    LUCKY
          I heard. Not exactly the Queen Mary.

                    DELGADO
          I am sorry it ends this way.

                    LUCKY
          It was always going to end some way.
          This way is better than most.

Lucky extends his hand through a gap in the chain link. Delgado reaches through and takes it.
They shake -- a firm, genuine grip. The same handshake as the airport lounge five months ago,
when Lucky sized up the Secret Police officer with the military bearing and the careful eyes.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          Take care of yourself, Carlos.

                    DELGADO
          And you, Senor --
               (catches himself)
          And you, Charlie.

Lucky almost smiles. He releases Delgado's hand.

Delgado steps back from the fence. He straightens his jacket -- the old habit, the professional
reflex of a man who has spent five months keeping his appearance sharp for a boss who noticed
everything.

He turns and walks toward the gate. His footsteps are steady. He doesn't look back.

Lucky watches him go. The last of his three visitors. Lansky, the friend. Genovese, the enemy.
Delgado -- the man who was both, and neither.

The gate opens. Delgado walks through. The gate closes.

Lucky stands at the fence in the late afternoon light. Alone. The sun touches the top of the
concrete wall and the compound fills with amber.

He turns and walks back to the barracks. A few more days. Then the ship. Then Italy. Then
whatever comes next for a man with an empire and no country to run it from.

## Production Notes
- Third and final Tiscornia visit scene -- each visit has a distinct emotional register (Lansky: friendship/loss,
Genovese: triumph/threat, Delgado: respect/farewell)
- This should be the quietest of the three -- minimal dialogue, maximum weight
- **TONAL NOTE (Act 3 Rhythm):** This scene should feel WARM despite the sadness -- respect, forgiveness, two men who
understand each other. Differentiate from Scene 26 (The Deportation) which should feel colder, more final, suffused with
loss and doubt. Scene 25 is about human connection; Scene 26 is about consequences. The warmth here makes Scene 26's
bleakness hit harder.
- Callback to Scene 5 (The New Home) and Scene 7 (Delgado Family Dinner) for the journey of this relationship
- The forgiveness from Lucky is essential to the thematic argument about "the devil you know"
