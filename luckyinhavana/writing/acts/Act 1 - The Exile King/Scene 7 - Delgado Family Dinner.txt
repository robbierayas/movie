# Scene 7 - Delgado Family Dinner

## LOCATION
INT. - DELGADO HOME - NIGHT (November 1946)

## CHARACTERS
Carlos Delgado, Maria Delgado, Delgado's Daughters, Extended Family

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
The Delgado home. Warm, modest, alive with family energy. Maria has prepared dinner. The daughters are at the table.
Extended family is present -- aunts, uncles, the cousin who lost his job. This is the world Delgado is protecting by
doing his job: a real family in a real home, far from the mansions and casinos of Lucky's orbit. The scene opens with
warmth and normalcy -- laughter, food, the comfortable noise of a family gathering.

### Key Beats
1. **The Good Provider**: Delgado has reason to boast. The new assignment pays well -- better money than regular police
work. New things for the house, something nice for Maria. He's proud he can provide for his family at a higher level. The
assignment has been a windfall, and the family is benefiting.
2. **The Cousin's Firing**: The topic surfaces over dinner. Delgado's cousin was fired from Oriental Park when Lucky's
American associates took over. The family asks Delgado to talk to his boss, use his connections, get the cousin his job
back. The implication is clear and uncomfortable: they think Delgado is connected. They think he's *in* with the mob.
They think he has pull with these people.
3. **Delgado Pushes Back**: Delgado gets upset. He's not in the mob -- he's a police officer doing his job. He's
protecting a VIP as assigned by his government. That's it. But the family doesn't see the difference. To them, he works
for Lucky Luciano. The better money, the new things for the house -- it all comes from the same place. The line between
"assigned to protect a mobster" and "working for a mobster" is one that Delgado can see but his family cannot.
4. **Alone After Dinner**: The family has gone. Delgado sits alone, stewing. The argument lingers. He can't fully argue
with them because they're not entirely wrong. The better paycheck, the nicer things, the proximity to power -- where does
duty end and complicity begin? He doesn't have an answer.

### Character Moments
- **Carlos Delgado**: The emotional core of the scene. Proud provider turned defensive son. He wants to give his family a
good life and believes he's doing honest work. But when his family holds up a mirror, he doesn't like what he sees. His
anger is really directed at himself -- at the uncomfortable truth that the line between police work and mob work has
blurred without him noticing.
- **Maria Delgado**: Practical, loving, perceptive. She sees the better money and appreciates it, but she also sees what
the family sees. Her concern isn't about morality -- it's about what this assignment is doing to her husband and where it
leads.
- **Delgado's Daughters**: Present but young, not fully understanding the adult tension. Their innocence is the silent
argument for everything Delgado does -- he provides for them, he protects them, and that's what matters. Or is it?
- **Extended Family**: The chorus of the scene. They represent the community's view: if you work for the mob, you're in
the mob. Their request about the cousin isn't malicious -- they genuinely believe Delgado has influence. Which makes it
worse.

### Closing
Delgado alone in the quiet house. The dishes cleared, the family gone or asleep. He sits with the weight of a question he
can't answer: where does the job end and the compromise begin? This is the scene that plants the seed of Delgado's
eventual alliance with Brennan -- the first crack in his loyalty to the assignment.

## FULL SCRIPT

INT. DELGADO HOME - KITCHEN / DINING ROOM - NIGHT (NOVEMBER 1946)

A modest Cuban home. Clean, warm, full of life. Tile floors, painted walls, a crucifix above the doorway. The kitchen
opens directly into the dining area where a long table is set for ten.

MARIA DELGADO, late 30s, dark-haired and capable, moves between stove and table with practiced efficiency. Steam rises
from pots. The smell of roast pork and black beans fills the house.

Sofia, 7, sets plates on the table with exaggerated care. ANA, 5, follows behind, placing napkins that immediately slide
off.

                    MARIA
          Sofia, help your sister with the napkins.

                    SOFIA
          She keeps putting them upside down.

                    ANA
          There's no upside down for napkins!

                    SOFIA
          Yes there is. Papa said so.

The front door opens. Delgado enters, still in his suit from work. Both girls rush him.

                    ANA
          Papa!

                    SOFIA
          Papa, tell Ana there's an upside down
          for napkins.

Delgado scoops Ana up with one arm and puts his free hand on Sofia's head.

                    DELGADO
          There is absolutely an upside down
          for napkins. But tonight, we don't care.

He kisses Maria. A brief, comfortable kiss -- the kind that comes from years together.

                    MARIA
          You're late.

                    DELGADO
          Traffic on the Malecon.

                    MARIA
          You're always late since the new
          assignment.

                    DELGADO
          The new assignment pays for the new
          curtains you wanted.

She gives him a look -- conceding the point but not the argument.

A KNOCK at the door. Maria opens it to DELGADO'S UNCLE TOMAS, 60s, barrel-chested and weathered, accompanied by AUNT
CARMEN, and behind them RAFAEL, Delgado's cousin, 30s, the one who lost his racetrack job. Rafael is clean-shaven,
wearing his best shirt, but there's something deflated about him.

Embraces, kisses on cheeks, the overlapping warmth of extended family arriving.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Carlos! Look at you. That suit is new.

                    DELGADO
          It's not new.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          It looks new.

Maria, calling from the kitchen.

                    MARIA
          It's new.

Laughter. Delgado loosens his tie and shakes his head.

INT. DELGADO HOME - DINING TABLE - LATER

The table is full. Family passes dishes, pours wine, talks over each other. The girls eat quietly, watching the adults.
It's loud, warm, alive -- the kind of dinner that defines a family.

                    DELGADO
          Maria, this pork is perfect.

                    MARIA
          It's the same pork I always make.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Then you always make perfect pork.

                    AUNT CARMEN
          Tomas, stop flirting with your
          nephew's wife.

More laughter. Delgado leans back, content. He watches his family with genuine warmth. This is what he works for. This
table, these people, this noise.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          So, Carlos. The new position. It
          agrees with you.

                    DELGADO
          It's a good assignment.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Important man you're protecting, from
          what I hear.

                    DELGADO
          An important visitor to Cuba. That's all.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          An important visitor who's taken over
          half the racetrack.

The warmth at the table shifts. Not dramatically -- but the temperature drops a degree. Rafael looks at his plate.

                    DELGADO
          Tio, let's not --

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Rafael has been at Oriental Park for
          eight years. Eight years. And last week
          they tell him he's finished. Americans
          are taking over his section. No notice,
          no severance, nothing.

Rafael, quiet.

                    RAFAEL
          Tio, it's all right.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          It's not all right. He has a wife. He
          has rent. You know how hard it is to
          find work right now?

Delgado sets his fork down carefully.

                    DELGADO
          I'm sorry about what happened to Rafael.
          But that has nothing to do with me.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Nothing to do with you? You work for
          the man whose people fired him.

                    DELGADO
          I don't work for him. I'm assigned to
          protect him. There's a difference.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Is there?

A beat. The table is quiet. Even the girls have stopped eating.

                    AUNT CARMEN
          Tomas --

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Carlos, I'm not attacking you. I'm
          asking you. You have a relationship
          with this man. You drive him, you're
          with him every day. Talk to him. Ask
          him to look into Rafael's situation.
          One word from you --

                    DELGADO
          I'm not going to do that.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          Why not?

                    DELGADO
          Because I am a police officer. I don't
          ask favors from the people I'm assigned
          to protect. That's not how it works.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          That is exactly how it works, Carlos.
          That is how everything in this country
          works. You know someone, you ask. That's
          Cuba.

                    DELGADO
          Not for me.

                    RAFAEL
          Carlos, forget it. I'll find something
          else. I don't want this to --

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          You shouldn't have to find something
          else. You had a job. These Americans
          come in and take what they want because
          they have money and connections. And
          your cousin has connections now.
          (to Delgado)
          Whether you like it or not.

Delgado pushes back from the table. Not violently -- but firmly. Everyone feels it.

                    DELGADO
          I am not connected. I am not part of
          this man's business. I carry a badge.
          I carry a government identification
          card. I report to the Secret Police
          command. That is what I am.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          And the new suit? And the new curtains
          Maria mentioned? Where does that money
          come from?

                    MARIA
          Tomas, that's enough.

                    UNCLE TOMAS
          It comes from the assignment. The
          assignment to protect a man whose people
          are taking jobs from your family.

Silence. Delgado's jaw tightens. He looks at Rafael, who won't meet his eyes. At Maria, whose expression holds both
sympathy and the quiet acknowledgment that Tomas isn't entirely wrong. At his daughters, who are watching with the wide,
uncertain eyes of children who feel tension they don't understand.

                    DELGADO
          I think dinner is over.

                    MARIA
          Carlos --

                    DELGADO
          Tomas, Carmen -- thank you for coming.
          Rafael, I'm sorry about the job. I
          genuinely am.

He stands and walks out of the room. The family sits in the aftermath. Maria closes her eyes briefly, then begins
clearing plates, because someone has to.

INT. DELGADO HOME - BACK PORCH - NIGHT

Small concrete porch. A clothesline, a single chair, the sounds of the neighborhood at night -- a radio playing
somewhere, a dog barking, distant traffic.

Delgado sits in the chair, smoking. He rarely smokes -- Maria doesn't like it. But tonight he needs something in his
hands.

The door opens. Maria comes out. She leans against the doorframe.

                    MARIA
          They've gone home.

Delgado nods.

                    MARIA (CONT'D)
          You know Tomas. He speaks before
          he thinks.

                    DELGADO
          He wasn't wrong.

Maria comes closer. She sits on the arm of his chair.

                    MARIA
          You're a good man, Carlos. And
          you're a good policeman.

                    DELGADO
          Those two things are getting harder
          to be at the same time.

She puts her hand on the back of his neck. They sit together in the dark.

                    MARIA
          The girls are asking why Uncle Tomas
          was upset.

                    DELGADO
          What did you tell them?

                    MARIA
          That adults sometimes disagree about
          things that aren't simple.

Delgado stubs out the cigarette.

                    DELGADO
          That's the best answer anyone's given
          tonight.

Maria goes back inside. Through the window, Delgado can see her putting the girls to bed -- tucking covers, smoothing
hair.

He sits alone. The question from dinner hangs in the air: where does the duty end and the compromise begin? He doesn't
have an answer. The cigarette smoke drifts into the dark.

## Production Notes
- Modest but warm Cuban home -- period-appropriate furnishings, family atmosphere
- This is the most intimate scene in Act 1 -- no gangsters, no casinos, just a family at dinner
- The tonal shift from the energy of the montage (Scene 6) to the quiet domesticity here is intentional
- Delgado's internal conflict must be visible without being stated outright -- the acting carries this
- The cousin should be present at dinner to make the firing personal and immediate
- Sets up Delgado's arc toward Scene 13 (Christmas Eve) where he agrees to help Brennan
- No Lucky, no Lansky, no Brennan -- this is Delgado's scene entirely
