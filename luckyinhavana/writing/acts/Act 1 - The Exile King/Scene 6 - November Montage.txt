# Scene 6 - November Montage (MONTAGE)

## TYPE
Linear Montage

## PURPOSE
Compresses the month of November 1946 to show Lucky's empire taking shape across Havana. Establishes the scale and pace
of his operations -- casinos, racetracks, the mansion as command center -- while planting seeds of the darker business
underneath (the docks meeting / heroin network) and the first personal cost landing close to Delgado (his cousin fired
from Oriental Park). Valdes is shown as the man greasing every wheel.

## TIME SPAN
Approximately one month (November 1946)

## MONTAGE STRUCTURE

### Shot 1
**Oriental Park Racetrack** -- Lucky at the Jockey Club tables, surrounded by well-dressed gamblers. Money changing
hands, races running, the energy of legal gambling operations in full swing. Lucky is in his element -- watching,
calculating, owning the room.

### Shot 2
**Casino Nacional / Hotel Nacional Casino** -- Lucky moving through the casino floor with Lansky or associates. Slot
machines, card tables, roulette wheels. The gambling empire expanding. Valdes appears, shaking hands, smoothing a permit,
making an introduction -- every door opens with an envelope.

### Shot 3
**Miramar Mansion** -- Lucky taking meetings at the mansion. Associates arriving, papers being signed, phone calls being
made. The mansion has become a command center. Staff serve drinks, Delgado stands watch. The machinery of an empire
running smoothly.

### Shot 4
**Havana Docks** -- The critical beat. **BREAK MONTAGE RHYTHM HERE: music drops out, pace slows, silence and tension
replace energy.** Lucky meets with SANTO TRAFFICANTE at the docks. Trafficante is the Tampa boss already embedded in
Cuba -- he knows the port, the shipping routes, the local contacts. Hushed conversation, coded language about "shipments"
and "routes." Crates and cargo in the background. Delgado is present as security but stands at a distance -- he doesn't
understand what he's witnessing. It looks like import/export business. He's uneasy but can't name why. This is the heroin
network being built. **This beat should play longer than any other shot in the montage -- let the silence do the work.**

### Shot 5
**Valdes Thriving** -- Valdes in his element: a government office, a restaurant, a car. The money flowing through Cuba
has never been better. He's the man in the middle of everything, taking his cut, facilitating every deal. A brief image
of Batista's Cuba at its most corrupt and prosperous.

### Shot 6
**Oriental Park -- Delgado's Cousin** -- Lucky's American associates taking over operations at the racetrack. New faces
replacing old ones. Delgado's cousin, who worked at the racetrack, being let go -- replaced by Americans. A brief,
wordless moment: the cousin walking away from the track, looking back. Delgado hears about it later but says nothing. The
first personal cost of Lucky's empire lands close to home.

## FULL SCRIPT

MONTAGE - LUCKY'S EMPIRE TAKES SHAPE (NOVEMBER 1946)

Cuban music rises -- a full son orchestra, brass and percussion, the heartbeat of Havana.

-- ORIENTAL PARK RACETRACK - DAY. Lucky sits at the Jockey Club tables, surrounded by well-dressed gamblers. He watches
the horses round the far turn, cigar in hand, utterly at ease. Money changes hands across the green felt. A CUBAN
BUSINESSMAN shakes Lucky's hand -- another contact made, another door opened.

-- CASINO NACIONAL - NIGHT. Lucky walks the casino floor with Lansky. Slot machines ring, roulette wheels spin, cards
snap against felt. VALDES appears from the crowd, guiding a nervous-looking GOVERNMENT OFFICIAL toward Lucky. Handshakes.
Smiles. Valdes slips an envelope into the official's breast pocket and pats it smooth. The man relaxes. Every door opens.

-- MIRAMAR MANSION - DAY. The mansion as command center. Lucky takes a meeting in the study -- three MEN IN SUITS spread
papers across a desk, pointing at maps. A PHONE RINGS and Lucky answers it, speaking rapid Italian. Delgado stands at the
door, watchful, a sentinel. Staff serve cafecito in small cups.

-- MIRAMAR MANSION - NIGHT. A different meeting. More men, more papers, more phone calls. The machinery of empire never
stops. Through the window, the ocean shimmers under moonlight.

-- HAVANA DOCKS - DAWN. The critical beat. Early morning fog. Cargo ships loom at anchor. Lucky stands on a wharf with
SANTO TRAFFICANTE, mid-50s, the Tampa boss already embedded in Cuba's port operations. Trafficante is all business -- he
knows these docks, knows the harbormaster, knows which ships don't get inspected. He walks Lucky through a shipping
manifest, tapping certain lines. Lucky nods. Trafficante produces a small sample -- a paper packet, white powder inside.
Lucky examines it, rubs a trace between his fingers, nods again. The packet disappears. Handshakes -- the grip of men
sealing something permanent. Delgado stands thirty feet away, by the car. He watches the meeting but hears nothing. His
face is still. But his eyes are uneasy. Something about this feels different from the racetracks and casinos. He can't
name it. He doesn't try.

-- VALDES'S OFFICE - DAY. Valdes at his desk, phone to his ear, signing documents without reading them. A stack of
PERMITS on his desk -- gaming licenses, import authorizations, building approvals. He stamps one after another. A
SECRETARY brings more. He stamps those too. Behind him, a framed portrait of President Batista watches from the wall.

-- ORIENTAL PARK RACETRACK - LATE AFTERNOON. New faces in the Jockey Club. AMERICAN ASSOCIATES -- Lucky's men from the
States -- take positions behind the tables. They move with an authority that displaces the old staff. DELGADO'S COUSIN,
30s, a racetrack worker in a white shirt, is stopped at a door he used to walk through. An American shakes his head. No.
The cousin stands there for a moment, disbelieving. Then he takes off his cap, looks back at the track one last time, and
walks away.

The music fades. The montage ends on:

INT. DELGADO'S CAR - EVENING

Delgado drives home alone. He passes through the working-class streets of his neighborhood -- a different Havana from the
mansions and casinos. He stops at a light. Stares at nothing.

He's heard about the cousin. He doesn't speak about it. But the weight of it sits on his face -- the first crack in the
simple story he's been telling himself about what this job is.

The light changes. He drives on.

CUT TO:

## Production Notes
- Multiple locations needed: Oriental Park Racetrack, casino interiors, Miramar mansion, Havana docks, government office
- **CRITICAL - The docks meeting (Shot 4) must BREAK the montage rhythm.** This is the most important plot point in
Act 1 -- the heroin network being established. The montage music should DROP OUT for this beat. Let the docks meeting
play in near-silence: fog, ambient harbor sounds, the scrape of cargo, low voices. Hold the shot longer than any other
beat in the montage. The sample exchange (white powder, nod) should play with tension and stillness, not montage energy.
After the beat concludes, the music can resume for Shots 5-6. The audience needs to feel the shift -- this isn't another
business deal, this is the thing that will destroy lives.
- Delgado's cousin firing (Shot 6) sets up Scene 7 (family dinner) -- this personal cost needs to register even in
montage
- Valdes should appear across multiple shots to emphasize his role as the facilitator of everything
- Music: 1940s Cuban music throughout -- establishing Havana's energy and rhythm. Exception: music drops for docks scene.
- Lighting progression could show passage of time: morning, afternoon, evening, night across the shots
