# Scene 10 - Arrival and Opening Night (MONTAGE)

## TYPE
Linear Montage with Voiceover Narration

## PURPOSE
Compress Brennan's rapid undercover setup in Havana and the arrival of 20+ mob bosses into a propulsive sequence.
Establishes the scope of the Havana Conference, Brennan's surveillance operation, and the lavish opening night -- all
filtered through Brennan's voiceover as he watches, documents, and pieces together what he's witnessing. Transitions the
film from the FBN's discovery in Act 1 to the conference itself in Act 2.

## TIME SPAN
Three days: December 20-22, 1946

## MONTAGE STRUCTURE

### Shot 1
INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - LOBBY - DAY. Brennan checks in under his cover identity. He scans the ornate lobby, taking in the
layout -- exits, elevators, the front desk. A man alone in a crowd, cataloging everything. **Near-miss with Lucky:** as
Brennan turns from the desk, Lucky crosses the lobby close enough to touch. Brennan sees the face of the man he blames
for his brother's death. Lucky doesn't see him. The invisible agent and the visible king, inches apart.

### Shot 2
INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - VARIOUS. Brennan recruits his informants: a hushed conversation with the telephone operator in a
service corridor, a folded bill passed to the elevator attendant. Quick, practiced moves. His network takes shape within
hours.

### Shot 3
INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BAR - EVENING. Brennan's first encounter with Delgado. They sit near each other at the hotel bar --
two men who don't yet know they'll become allies. Brennan clocks Delgado's Secret Police bearing; Delgado notices Brennan
watching. Neither speaks. A charged moment of mutual assessment.

### Shot 4
INT. HOTEL ROOM / PHONE. Brennan calls Hartley. Reports hotel layout, initial observations, conference imminent. Hartley
gives tactical guidance, asks probing questions about who's arriving. (Hartley already knows much of this from Genovese
-- he's confirming what his mob source told him.)

### Shot 5
EXT./INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - ARRIVALS. The mob bosses arrive in a parade of luxury. Black cars pulling up to the hotel
entrance. Frank Costello stepping out in an immaculate suit. Vito Genovese scanning the lobby with cold eyes. Albert
Anastasia, thick-necked and intimidating. Joe Bonanno, Santo Trafficante, the Fischetti Brothers, Joe Adonis, Willie
Moretti -- face after face. Brennan watches from a distance, identifying each one, scribbling names in a notebook.

### Shot 6
INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BALLROOM - NIGHT. Opening night. The ballroom is transformed -- lavish dinner tables, crystal,
flowers, Cuban music. Frank Sinatra takes the stage. The cover story made real: a celebrity Christmas party. Twenty of
America's most dangerous criminals in tuxedos, their wives in evening gowns, applauding Sinatra as if they're at the
Copa.

### Shot 7
INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BALLROOM / PRIVATE AREA - NIGHT. "Christmas presents" are exchanged -- envelopes containing
$200,000+ passed between bosses with handshakes and embraces. The Fischetti Brothers arrive with a suitcase. It's opened
in a private room: $2 million in cash. Lucky presides over it all -- the exiled king holding court, more powerful than
ever.

### Shot 8
INT. HOTEL ROOM - NIGHT. Brennan alone, writing his report by lamplight. His voiceover gives scope and weight to what
he's witnessed: every major criminal in America, in one room, in a country where none of this is illegal. He's in over
his head and he knows it. But this is what he came for.

## FULL SCRIPT

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - LOBBY - DAY (DECEMBER 20, 1946)

A magnificent colonial lobby. Marble floors, palm fronds, ceiling fans
turning slow. Porters in white jackets attend to wealthy guests.

JACK BRENNAN, late 30s, hard-jawed, wearing a linen suit that doesn't
quite fit the surroundings, approaches the front desk with a leather
suitcase. His eyes sweep the room -- cataloging exits, sightlines, the
elevator bank.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          I checked into the Hotel Nacional on
          December twentieth under the name
          James Burke, textile buyer from
          Philadelphia. Room 412, fourth floor.
          The bosses were on the eighth. Three
          hundred rooms in that hotel, and these
          men had spent their whole lives
          believing Cuba was untouchable. They
          weren't looking for surveillance.
          They weren't looking at me. They were
          looking at who was coming.

He signs the register. Takes his key. His gaze lingers on the grand
staircase, the balcony overlooking the lobby.

He turns from the desk and nearly collides with a man crossing the
lobby. Dark overcoat, hat tilted at a precise angle, two men flanking
him. LUCKY LUCIANO walks past Brennan close enough to touch -- close
enough that Brennan catches the scent of his cologne.

Lucky doesn't look at him. Why would he? A textile buyer from
Philadelphia is invisible.

Brennan stands frozen. Watching Lucky cross the lobby and disappear
into the elevator with Lansky. The doors close. Gone.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          That was the first time I saw him in
          person since the courthouse steps.
          Close enough to reach out and touch
          the man I blamed for my brother's
          death. He didn't see me. He never
          saw me. That was the whole point.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - SERVICE CORRIDOR - DAY

Brennan walks alongside ROSA, 40s, the hotel telephone operator, in a
narrow service hallway. He speaks low, his hand pressing a folded bill
into her palm.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Rosa at the switchboard. Every call
          in and out of the hotel went through
          her. Twenty dollars a week bought me
          a list of every number dialed from
          the eighth floor.

Rosa tucks the money into her apron without breaking stride.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - ELEVATOR - DAY

Brennan steps into the elevator. ERNESTO, 20s, the attendant, pulls
the brass gate shut. Brennan palms him a bill with a handshake.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Ernesto ran the elevator. He knew
          every face, every floor, who visited
          whom and when. Cost me ten dollars
          and a promise I'd get his cousin a
          visa. I lied about the visa.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BAR - EVENING

Amber light. A mahogany bar with Art Deco mirrors. CARLOS DELGADO,
early 40s, sits alone with a glass of rum, his posture rigid, his suit
too well-pressed for a tourist. He watches the room with trained eyes.

Brennan settles onto a stool two seats away. Orders a bourbon. Their
eyes meet in the mirror behind the bar. A beat of mutual assessment.

Delgado clocks Brennan's watchfulness. Brennan clocks Delgado's
bearing -- law enforcement, not mob. Neither speaks. Delgado returns
to his drink. Brennan does the same.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          There was a man at the bar I couldn't
          place. Not a guest. Not staff. He had
          the look of someone watching the same
          things I was watching. I didn't know
          his name yet. I'd learn it soon enough.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BRENNAN'S ROOM - NIGHT

Brennan sits on the bed, phone receiver pressed to his ear. A notepad
on his knee, hotel blueprints spread on the bedspread.

                    BRENNAN
          I'm in. Room 412. The hotel's set up
          on a grid -- eighth floor reserved
          for VIPs, ballroom on the ground
          level, service access through the
          basement. I've got eyes on the
          switchboard and the elevator.

INTERCUT WITH:

INT. FBN HEADQUARTERS - HARTLEY'S OFFICE - NIGHT

DIRECTOR WILLIAM HARTLEY, late 50s, silver-haired, sits behind a
massive desk. An American flag behind him. He holds the phone with
practiced ease.

                    HARTLEY
          Good. Who's arrived?

                    BRENNAN
          Nobody yet. But the eighth floor's
          been cleared and locked. They're
          expecting a large party. Very large.

                    HARTLEY
          How large?

                    BRENNAN
          They've reserved the entire floor.
          Twenty-plus rooms. Something's coming,
          Director. Something big.

Hartley, a beat -- he already knows.

                    HARTLEY
          Keep your eyes open, Jack. Names and
          faces. Document everything. I want to
          know who walks through that door.

Brennan hangs up. Stares at the phone.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Hartley told me to watch. So I
          watched.

EXT. HOTEL NACIONAL - ENTRANCE - DAY (DECEMBER 21, 1946)

A black Cadillac pulls to the curb. A DOORMAN opens the rear door.

FRANK COSTELLO steps out -- immaculate dark suit, silk tie, fedora
tilted at a precise angle. He adjusts his cuffs and walks inside like
he owns the building.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          December twenty-first. They started
          coming.

EXT. HOTEL NACIONAL - ENTRANCE - CONTINUOUS

Another car. VITO GENOVESE emerges -- stocky, coiled, scanning the
surroundings with cold, appraising eyes before he moves.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Costello. Genovese.

EXT. HOTEL NACIONAL - ENTRANCE - CONTINUOUS

ALBERT ANASTASIA climbs out of a sedan -- thick neck, heavy shoulders,
a face that discourages conversation. He doesn't look around. He
doesn't need to.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Anastasia.

MONTAGE - ARRIVALS (DECEMBER 21-22)

-- JOE BONANNO arriving with a leather briefcase.
-- SANTO TRAFFICANTE stepping from a taxi, squinting in the sun.
-- THE FISCHETTI BROTHERS, laughing, carrying luggage.
-- JOE ADONIS adjusting his hat.
-- WILLIE MORETTI greeting another boss with a bear hug.

Each arrival, Brennan watches from behind a newspaper in the lobby, or
from a second-floor balcony, or through the window of the bar. A
notebook in his lap, names written in a careful hand.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Bonanno. Trafficante. The Fischetti
          boys. Adonis. Moretti. Twenty bosses
          from every territory in America,
          checking into the same hotel in
          Havana over forty-eight hours. If you
          dropped a bomb on the Nacional that
          week, organized crime in America
          would've been finished. Nobody
          dropped a bomb.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BALLROOM - NIGHT (DECEMBER 22, 1946)

The ballroom blazes with light. Crystal chandeliers. Round dinner
tables draped in white linen, set with silver and flowers. A BAND
plays Cuban jazz. WOMEN in evening gowns and MEN in tuxedos fill the
room -- the cream of American organized crime, dressed for a Christmas
party.

At a microphone on the low stage, FRANK SINATRA, 31, lean and
electric, launches into a song. His voice fills the room. The
audience applauds.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Opening night. The cover story was a
          Christmas party hosted by the
          Fischettis, with entertainment
          provided by Frank Sinatra himself.
          And there he was -- the Voice --
          singing for a room full of men who'd
          ordered more killings than the
          Pacific theater.

Sinatra croons. The bosses and their wives sway. LUCKY LUCIANO sits
at the head table with MEYER LANSKY beside him. Lucky is relaxed,
regal -- a king at his own banquet. Lansky leans over, murmurs
something. Lucky smiles.

Genovese watches from a nearby table, his expression controlled,
unreadable.

Anastasia laughs loudly at something Costello says, his meaty hand
slapping the table.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BALLROOM / CORRIDOR - NIGHT

The Fischetti Brothers escort a MAN carrying a heavy SUITCASE through
a side door into a private room off the ballroom. The door closes.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - PRIVATE ROOM - CONTINUOUS

The suitcase lands on a table. Clicks open. Stacks of cash -- neat,
banded, filling the case. Two million dollars.

Lucky looks at it. Nods. Lansky is already counting.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Christmas presents. Envelopes with
          two hundred thousand or more passed
          hand to hand like greeting cards. And
          the main event -- a suitcase with two
          million in cash delivered by the
          Fischettis. I didn't see the money.
          But I knew it was there. Rosa heard
          the calls. Ernesto saw the suitcase.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BALLROOM - NIGHT

Back in the ballroom, bosses embrace and exchange envelopes -- thick,
white, sealed. Handshakes linger. Smiles that mean business.

Lucky stands, raises his glass. The room quiets. Twenty of the most
powerful criminals in the Western Hemisphere look at one man.

                    LUCKY
          Salute.

They drink. Sinatra starts another song.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BRENNAN'S ROOM - NIGHT

Brennan alone. A single lamp. He writes in his notebook, transferring
scribbled observations into organized notes. Names. Room numbers.
Connections drawn with lines.

He stops writing. Sets down his pen. Looks at the wall.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          Every major criminal in America. In
          one room. In a country where none of
          this is illegal. I was one man with a
          notebook and a telephone operator.
          And I was all the United States
          government had in that building.

He picks up the pen. Goes back to writing.

                    BRENNAN (V.O.)
          So I kept writing.

## Production Notes
- Brennan's voiceover carries the entire montage -- his voice provides continuity across all shots
- Sinatra performance should feel authentic to 1946 (period-appropriate songs, stage setup)
- The $2 million suitcase reveal is a key visual -- needs dramatic framing
- Mob boss arrivals should feel like a parade of power: each arrival distinct, Brennan watching from a vantage point
- The bar encounter between Brennan and Delgado is wordless but charged -- plant the seed for their later alliance
- Tone shifts from Brennan's controlled professionalism to the overwhelming scale of what he's witnessing
