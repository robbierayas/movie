# Scene 14 - Christmas Day

## LOCATION
INT. - HOTEL NACIONAL - DAY/NIGHT (December 25-26, 1946)

## CHARACTERS
Lucky Luciano, Vito Genovese, Meyer Lansky, Conference Bosses

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
Christmas Day at the Hotel Nacional. The conference is in its final sessions. The mood is a mix of holiday celebration
and unfinished business. The bosses are preparing to depart Havana and return to their territories with the agreements
made during the week. But two critical threads are still in motion: Genovese's secret treachery and the Flamingo's fate.

### Key Beats
1. **Genovese's secret calls**: Genovese is seen making phone calls in private -- hushed, furtive, away from the other
bosses. He is feeding intelligence to the FBN, reporting on the conference, on Lucky's operations, on everything he's
witnessed. This is his long game: if he can't beat Lucky in a vote, he'll use the United States government to destroy
him. The FBN becomes Genovese's weapon. The audience sees what Brennan and Hartley don't yet understand -- the
information pipeline that will ultimately drive Lucky's deportation.
2. **The Flamingo disaster**: News arrives from Las Vegas. The Flamingo Hotel has opened on December 26th, and it's a
catastrophe. Celebrities stayed away. Gamblers lost big and the house couldn't cover it. The hotel wings aren't even
finished -- guests can hear construction. The opening-night losses are staggering. The bosses who invested millions are
furious. Siegel's stay of execution just got a lot shorter.
3. **Lucky saves Siegel (for now)**: Lucky uses his authority and his freshly reaffirmed status as Boss of All Bosses to
convince the Commission to give Siegel until spring. If the Flamingo can't turn around by then, Siegel is finished.
Lucky's argument is pragmatic: killing Siegel now means writing off the entire investment. Give him time, and maybe the
money comes back. The bosses agree reluctantly. (Historically, Siegel will be murdered in June 1947.)

### Character Moments
- **Vito Genovese**: Reveals the depth of his treachery. While the other bosses debate business openly, Genovese is
working in the shadows -- feeding the very government that will destroy their operation. His ambition has no limits and
no loyalty. He will use anyone and anything to seize power. The secret phone calls are the most dangerous moments of the
entire conference, and no one in the room knows it.
- **Lucky Luciano**: Demonstrates leadership even in a losing situation. The Flamingo is a disaster that reflects on him
-- he argued for patience, and patience hasn't paid off yet. But he holds the room, makes the case for more time, and
gets his way. His authority is intact, but the cracks are showing.
- **Meyer Lansky**: Receives the Flamingo news with the controlled devastation of a man watching his childhood friend's
death warrant being written. He knows the numbers. He knows what the other bosses will demand if things don't improve. He
supports Lucky's argument for time because time is all he can give Siegel.

### Closing
The conference ends. The bosses depart Havana with their territories, their heroin routes, and their agreements. Lucky
has held power, but Genovese's secret calls have set forces in motion that no one at the conference can see. The Flamingo
disaster hangs over Siegel like a death sentence. Christmas in Havana ends not with peace but with countdown clocks --
for Siegel, for Lucky, for all of them.

## FULL SCRIPT

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - CORRIDOR - EARLY MORNING (DECEMBER 25, 1946)

Christmas morning. The hotel is quiet. Garlands and holly drape the
hallway sconces. A room service cart sits abandoned outside a door.

VITO GENOVESE walks down the corridor in his shirtsleeves, checking
over his shoulder. He finds an empty service alcove near the back
stairwell. A telephone on the wall.

He picks up the receiver. Dials. Waits. Checks the corridor again.

Genovese, low, controlled.

                    GENOVESE
          It's me. I don't have long.

He cups his hand around the mouthpiece.

                    GENOVESE (CONT'D)
          The conference is winding down. I've
          got everything. Attendees, the
          structure, the supply routes.
          Luciano's running the whole operation
          from here -- heroin, gambling, all of
          it. He's got the Corsicans building a
          pipeline from France.

He listens.

                    GENOVESE (CONT'D)
          Every boss in America was in that
          room. I can give you names, dates,
          territories. Everything your director
          needs to make a case.

He listens again. A thin smile crosses his face.

                    GENOVESE (CONT'D)
          You tell Hartley that Luciano is
          running a narcotics empire from a
          mansion in Havana. That's all he
          needs to hear. The rest is details.

He glances down the corridor. A MAID pushes a cart around the far
corner. Genovese turns away from her, lowers his voice further.

                    GENOVESE (CONT'D)
          I'll call again when I'm back in New
          York. Merry Christmas.

He hangs up. Straightens his collar. Walks back down the corridor as
if he's just been out for a morning stroll.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - LOBBY - DAY (DECEMBER 26, 1946)

The day after Christmas. Suitcases in the lobby. PORTERS loading
luggage onto carts. Some bosses are already departing -- handshakes,
embraces, promises to stay in touch.

Lucky stands near the front desk with Lansky, seeing off guests.
Costello approaches, adjusts his coat.

                    COSTELLO
          Hell of a week, Charlie.

                    LUCKY
          Come back anytime, Frank. The weather's
          better than New York.

Costello smiles.

                    COSTELLO
          The company's about the same.

They shake hands. Costello walks out. Behind him, Anastasia claps
Lucky on the shoulder.

                    ANASTASIA
          You need me, you call. Day or night.

                    LUCKY
          I know, Albert. Safe trip home.

Anastasia nods and follows the porters out.

TRAFFICANTE approaches Lansky, shakes his hand.

                    TRAFFICANTE
          Meyer, when the pipeline's ready,
          Tampa's open. Whatever you need moved
          through my port.

                    LANSKY
          I'll be in touch.

The lobby empties. The conference is over. The last cars pull away
from the hotel entrance.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - LANSKY'S SUITE - EVENING

Lansky sits in an armchair, the evening paper spread before him. Lucky
paces by the window, a drink in his hand. The suite feels empty after
days of crowded rooms.

A KNOCK. Lucky opens the door. MORETTI stands there, agitated,
holding a telegram.

                    MORETTI
          You're gonna want to see this.

He hands the telegram to Lucky. Lucky reads it. His jaw tightens.

                    LUCKY
          Meyer.

He hands it to Lansky. Lansky reads. His face goes very still.

                    LANSKY
          Opening night. Disaster.

                    MORETTI
          The celebrities didn't show.
          Gamblers cleaned out the house and
          the reserves couldn't cover it. Half
          the hotel isn't even finished --
          people could hear construction from
          their rooms. It's a catastrophe.

                    LUCKY
          How bad?

                    MORETTI
          They're talking about shutting it
          down within two weeks. The losses
          from one night --

He shakes his head.

                    MORETTI (CONT'D)
          It's bad, Charlie. Real bad.

Lucky turns to Lansky. Lansky is still staring at the telegram. His
expression hasn't changed, but his hand, holding the paper, has gone
white at the knuckles.

                    LUCKY
          Meyer.

Lansky, quietly.

                    LANSKY
          I heard.

                    MORETTI
          The boys are gonna want blood. You
          know that. Six million of their money
          in a building that can't even keep
          its lights on.

                    LUCKY
          Nobody moves on Ben.

                    MORETTI
          Charlie --

                    LUCKY
          Nobody. Moves. On Ben. Not yet.

He takes the telegram from Lansky. Folds it. Puts it in his pocket.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          We gave him until the opening. The
          opening was a disaster. Fine. But the
          Flamingo's still standing. The tables
          are still there. The rooms are still
          there. You kill the man, you kill the
          project. Six million, gone. No chance
          of getting any of it back.

                    MORETTI
          And if it doesn't turn around?

                    LUCKY
          Then it doesn't turn around. And
          we'll handle it. But not now. Not
          over one bad night.

He looks at Moretti.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          You pass that along. To everybody.
          Siegel gets until spring. If the
          Flamingo can't make money by then,
          I'll sign the order myself.

Moretti looks at Lansky. Lansky nods almost imperceptibly.

                    MORETTI
          All right. Spring.

He leaves. The door closes.

Lucky and Lansky are alone. A long silence.

                    LANSKY
          It won't turn around.

                    LUCKY
          Maybe not.

                    LANSKY
          Ben was always -- he could sell
          anything. The big ideas, the vision.
          But the details... the details
          always killed him.

                    LUCKY
          I bought you time, Meyer. That's all
          I can do.

Lansky folds his hands. Stares at nothing.

                    LANSKY
          I know.

Lucky finishes his drink. Sets the glass down.

                    LUCKY
          This conference. The vote.
          Genovese. The pipeline. Siegel. And
          now this.

He walks to the window. Looks out at the Havana night. Lights on the
water. Music from somewhere far away.

                    LUCKY (CONT'D)
          Five days. We built an empire and
          watched it crack in the same week.

                    LANSKY
          It hasn't cracked. The pipeline's
          solid. The territories are set. The
          vote held. Siegel's a side issue.

                    LUCKY
          Siegel's not the crack I'm worried
          about.

Lansky looks at him. Understands.

                    LANSKY
          Genovese.

                    LUCKY
          He voted with us. He shook my hand.
          And I don't trust him one inch further
          than I can see him.

                    LANSKY
          Neither do I.

Lucky turns from the window.

                    LUCKY
          Keep your eyes open, Meyer. Watch who
          he talks to. Watch where his money
          goes. A man that hungry doesn't stop
          eating just because you told him to.

                    LANSKY
          I will.

Lucky nods. Walks to the door. Stops.

                    LUCKY
          Merry Christmas, Meyer.

                    LANSKY
          Merry Christmas, Charlie.

Lucky leaves. Lansky sits alone in the suite. He picks up the
telegram. Reads it one more time. Then he folds it very carefully
and puts it in his breast pocket, close to his chest.

He reaches for the Scotch. Pours himself a glass. Drinks it slowly,
looking at nothing.

## Production Notes
- Genovese's phone calls should be shot to emphasize secrecy and duplicity -- a man alone in a corridor or empty room,
speaking in low tones, checking over his shoulder
- The Flamingo disaster news should feel like a blow to the room -- the bosses' investment just went up in smoke
- The contrast between Christmas atmosphere and criminal business provides ongoing ironic tension
- This scene closes out the conference proper -- the departures should feel like an end-of-era moment, the last time all
these men are together
- Genovese's treachery is revealed to the audience but NOT to the other characters -- dramatic irony that will pay off in
Act 3
