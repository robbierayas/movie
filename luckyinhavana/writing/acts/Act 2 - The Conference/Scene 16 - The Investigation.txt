# Scene 16 - The Investigation

## LOCATION
INT. - HOTEL NACIONAL / VARIOUS HAVANA LOCATIONS - DAY (January 1947)

## CHARACTERS
Jack Brennan, Carlos Delgado, Director Hartley (via phone)

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
January 1947. The conference is over, the bosses are gone, but the evidence remains. Brennan has been compiling
intelligence from his informants and from Delgado -- piecing together the full picture of what was discussed and decided
at the Havana Conference. This is the investigative heart of the film: a man building a case from fragments.

### Key Beats
1. **Brennan compiles the evidence**: Brennan documents the complete heroin network as discussed at the conference -- the
French Connection pipeline from Turkey and Lebanon through Sicily to Southern France to Cuba to U.S. ports. He has names,
routes, territory divisions, projected revenues. His informants at the Hotel Nacional provided the framework; Delgado
filled in the details from his proximity to Lucky. The case against Luciano is taking shape.
2. **First call to Hartley -- the debrief**: Brennan calls Hartley with a full conference debrief: attendees, heroin
routes, territory divisions. Hartley praises him -- "This is exactly what we needed, Jack." Says Washington is building
the deportation case. Brennan feels validated, like his work matters. What he doesn't know: Hartley already had much of
this from Genovese. Brennan's reports just confirm what Genovese fed them, making the case airtight.
3. **The anomaly Brennan notices**: Something odd: some of Brennan's intelligence seems to already be known in Washington
before he reports it. Details he discovers are met with unsurprised responses. He files it away -- trusts Hartley,
assumes there are other sources he doesn't know about. He's right about other sources. He's wrong about what that means.
4. **Second call to Hartley -- Genovese thread**: Brennan reports on Genovese's behavior -- the man is making moves,
undermining Lucky, acting suspiciously. Hartley deflects immediately: "Stay focused on Luciano. Genovese isn't your
assignment." The dismissal is sharp enough to notice but not sharp enough to alarm. Hartley can't have Brennan pulling at
the Genovese thread because Genovese is his source.
5. **The alliance deepens**: Delgado and Brennan meet regularly, sharing information and building trust. Their
conversations go beyond intelligence exchange -- they debate the larger implications of what they're doing. Delgado has
been close to Lucky for months; he's seen the man's restraint, his code, the way he operates. He's also seen Genovese --
the ambition without limits, the ruthlessness without rules. Delgado raises the question that will define the film's
moral center: are they making things worse by targeting Lucky instead of Genovese?

### Character Moments
- **Jack Brennan**: At his most competent and most vulnerable. The investigation is solid -- he's a good agent doing good
work. But the seeds of the film's tragedy are planted here: the intelligence anomaly he notices and dismisses, the
Genovese thread Hartley shuts down, the growing doubt that Delgado's questions introduce. Brennan trusts the system
because he needs to -- his brother's death demands that this work mean something.
- **Carlos Delgado**: Emerges as the moral conscience of the film. His proximity to Lucky has given him insight the
others lack: Lucky has restraint, Genovese has none. Remove Lucky and you don't stop the heroin -- you just hand it to
someone worse. Delgado's warning -- "You're creating a bigger monster" -- is the film's thesis spoken aloud. He's not
wrong, but he keeps helping Brennan anyway, because the assignment is the assignment.
- **Director Hartley (via phone)**: The voice of institutional corruption disguised as authority. His praise of Brennan
is genuine -- Brennan is doing excellent work. His dismissal of the Genovese thread is calculated -- he can't let Brennan
discover his source. Hartley occupies the moral blind spot where ambition and duty become indistinguishable.

### Closing
Brennan has his case. Delgado has his doubts. Hartley has his agenda. The investigation moves forward on parallel tracks
that the characters can't see converging. Brennan believes he's building a case to stop a drug lord. Hartley is building
a career-making win. Genovese is building a path to the throne. And Delgado sees it all but can't stop any of it. The
scene ends with Brennan satisfied and Delgado troubled -- the truth is closer to Delgado's unease than to Brennan's
confidence.

## FULL SCRIPT

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BRENNAN'S ROOM - DAY (JANUARY 1947)

TITLE CARD: JANUARY 1947

Brennan's hotel room has become an operations center. The bed is
pushed against the wall. A desk is covered with notes, photographs,
a map of the Caribbean pinned to a corkboard. Red string connects
names to locations. A hand-drawn diagram shows the heroin pipeline:
TURKEY --> SICILY --> FRANCE --> CUBA --> U.S.

JACK BRENNAN sits at the desk, organizing his files. He writes in a
leather notebook with methodical precision. Pages and pages of names,
dates, room numbers.

He picks up a photograph -- a telephoto shot of Lucky Luciano at the
Hotel Nacional entrance with Lansky. Pins it to the corkboard.

Another photograph -- Genovese arriving by car. Pins that too.

He draws a line between Lucky and Lansky. Another between Lucky and
the word "HEROIN." He steps back and looks at the web of connections.

                         BRENNAN (V.O.)
              Three weeks after the conference.
              The bosses were gone, but I had
              everything. Names, routes, territories,
              revenue projections. Enough to build a
              case that would put Luciano away for
              the rest of his life. If he were on
              American soil.

He picks up the phone.

INT. FBN HEADQUARTERS - HARTLEY'S OFFICE - DAY

INTERCUT WITH BRENNAN'S HOTEL ROOM:

DIRECTOR HARTLEY answers on the second ring.

                         HARTLEY
              Brennan.

                         BRENNAN
              Director. I'm ready for the full
              debrief.

                         HARTLEY
              Go ahead.

                         BRENNAN
              Havana Conference, December twenty-two
              through twenty-six. Twenty-three
              confirmed attendees. Luciano, Lansky,
              Genovese, Anastasia, Costello, Bonanno,
              Trafficante, Adonis, Moretti, the
              Fischettis --

                         HARTLEY
              I've got the attendee list. Give me
              the substance.

Brennan pauses. A flicker of something -- surprise that Hartley
already has a list he hasn't yet submitted.

                         BRENNAN
              The heroin network. They're replacing
              the China route with a new pipeline.
              Opium from Turkey and Lebanon,
              processed in Sicily, refined in
              Marseille by Corsican operators, then
              shipped to Havana. From Cuba,
              distribution to Tampa, New Orleans,
              and New York. They're calling it the
              French Connection.

                         HARTLEY
              Revenue projections?

                         BRENNAN
              Fifty million a year. Full capacity
              by end of forty-seven.

                         HARTLEY
              Territory divisions?

                         BRENNAN
              Tampa goes to Trafficante. New
              Orleans is shared. New York divided
              among the five families. Luciano and
              Lansky control the Cuba hub.

A beat.

                         HARTLEY
              This is exactly what we needed, Jack.
              Outstanding work. Washington is
              building the deportation case as we
              speak. Your evidence is the backbone.

                         BRENNAN
              Thank you, Director.

                         HARTLEY
              Keep building the file. Names,
              photographs, financial records --
              anything you can document. When we
              move on this, I want it airtight.

                         BRENNAN
              Understood. One thing, Director.

                         HARTLEY
              What is it?

                         BRENNAN
              The attendee list. How did you --

                         HARTLEY
              Multiple sources, Jack. You're not
              our only asset in the Caribbean.
              Don't worry about where the rest
              comes from. Worry about making your
              piece bulletproof.

                         BRENNAN
              Yes, sir.

He hangs up. Sits back. Looks at the phone.

                         BRENNAN (V.O.)
              He already had the list. I hadn't
              filed it yet, and he already had it.
              Multiple sources, he said. Fair
              enough. I filed it away and kept
              working.

EXT. MIRAMAR MANSION - LUCKY'S STUDY - NIGHT

Delgado stands outside the study door. Inside, Lucky is on the phone
-- rapid Italian, a name Delgado recognizes: Trafficante. Through the
crack in the door, he can see papers on the desk. A shipping manifest
with dock numbers and dates. A telephone number written on hotel
stationery.

Lucky's voice rises, then drops. Delgado glances down the hall. The
houseman is in the kitchen. No one is watching.

He steps into the doorway, pretending to check the room. His eyes
sweep the desk. He memorizes the dock numbers. The telephone number.
A name he hasn't seen before -- a Corsican name, written in Lansky's
handwriting.

Lucky turns from the phone. Sees Delgado.

                         LUCKY
              Something wrong?

                         DELGADO
              Checking the windows. Routine.

                         LUCKY
              You're a good man, Carlos.

Delgado nods. Steps back into the hall. His heart is pounding but his
face shows nothing. He walks to the bathroom, locks the door, and
writes the dock numbers and the telephone number on a scrap of paper
from his pocket.

EXT. HAVANA - WATERFRONT CAFE - DAY

Brennan and DELGADO sit at an outdoor table overlooking the harbor.
Coffee cups. Brennan has a small notebook open. Delgado speaks in a
low voice, his eyes scanning the passing pedestrians.

Delgado slides a folded scrap of paper across the table. Brennan
opens it. Dock numbers. A phone number. A Corsican name.

                         DELGADO
              The supply routes -- I heard them
              discussed at the conference. But
              what I've seen at the docks is
              different. Luciano has been meeting
              with Corsicans since November.
              Sicilians too. These aren't
              hypotheticals. The pipeline is
              already being built.

                         BRENNAN
              You've seen the dock meetings?

                         DELGADO
              I've been standing ten feet away
              while they happened. I didn't
              understand what I was looking at
              until the conference made it clear.
              Import-export meetings, they call
              them. Very polite. Very quiet. The
              cargo manifests are fiction.

Brennan writes.

                         BRENNAN
              I need details. Dates, names,
              vessels.

                         DELGADO
              I'll get you what I can. But Brennan
              -- there's something else.

He leans forward.

                         DELGADO (CONT'D)
              Someone tried to kill Luciano last
              week. Gunmen at his mansion.
              Professionals.

                         BRENNAN
              I heard rumors. Any leads?

                         DELGADO
              The cars had New York plates. And
              Genovese left Havana two days before
              it happened.

                         BRENNAN
              You think Genovese ordered it?

                         DELGADO
              I think Genovese lost a vote and got
              his hand shaken in front of twenty
              bosses. And I think a man like that
              doesn't forget.

Brennan considers this.

                         BRENNAN
              Genovese is secondary. Luciano's the
              target.

                         DELGADO
              That's your assignment. I understand.
              But I'm telling you something you
              don't want to hear.

He looks Brennan in the eye.

                         DELGADO (CONT'D)
              I've been with Luciano for three
              months. I've watched him run his
              operation. He has rules. He has a
              code. He doesn't move on people
              without reason, and he doesn't let
              his men act like animals. Genovese --

He shakes his head.

                         DELGADO (CONT'D)
              Genovese has no rules. None. I've
              seen the way he looks at people. Like
              they're furniture. Like they're
              nothing. You take Luciano out of the
              picture, Genovese fills the hole.
              And he fills it with something worse.

                         BRENNAN
              My brother died with a needle in his
              arm, Delgado. Luciano's heroin.
              Luciano's network. I'm not here to
              debate which mobster is the lesser
              evil.

                         DELGADO
              I'm not asking you to. I'm telling
              you what I see. You remove Luciano,
              the heroin doesn't stop. It gets
              worse. You're not stopping a monster.
              You're creating a bigger one.

A beat. The words land. Brennan stares at his notebook.

                         BRENNAN
              That's not my call to make.

                         DELGADO
              Whose call is it?

                         BRENNAN
              Washington's. I gather the evidence.
              They make the decisions. That's how
              it works.

                         DELGADO
              And if Washington is wrong?

Brennan doesn't answer. He closes his notebook.

INT. HOTEL NACIONAL - BRENNAN'S ROOM - NIGHT

Brennan on the phone again. This call is different -- his voice is
sharper, pushing.

                         BRENNAN
              Director, I need to flag something
              about Genovese.

INTERCUT WITH:

INT. FBN HEADQUARTERS - HARTLEY'S OFFICE - NIGHT

Hartley at his desk, papers spread before him. He sets down his pen.

                         HARTLEY
              What about him?

                         BRENNAN
              His behavior at the conference was
              openly antagonistic toward Luciano.
              He challenged the vote, got shut
              down, and was forced into a public
              handshake. Two days after he left
              Havana, there was an assassination
              attempt on Luciano's mansion. Cars
              with New York plates.

                         HARTLEY
              That's speculation.

                         BRENNAN
              It's a pattern. Genovese is making
              moves. He's undermining Luciano from
              the inside. My source says --

                         HARTLEY
              Your source.

                         BRENNAN
              My Cuban contact. He's been on
              Luciano's detail for months. He says
              Genovese is more dangerous than
              Luciano. That if we remove Luciano --

                         HARTLEY
                    (sharp)
              Jack. Stop.

A beat.

                         HARTLEY (CONT'D)
              Your assignment is Lucky Luciano.
              Not Genovese. Not the internal
              politics of the Mafia. Luciano.
              He's building a narcotics empire
              ninety miles from the United States.
              That's what we're stopping. Stay
              focused.

                         BRENNAN
              Director, if Genovese is positioning
              himself to --

                         HARTLEY
              Genovese is not your assignment.

The words are a wall. Brennan stops.

                         HARTLEY (CONT'D)
              You've done excellent work, Jack.
              Don't dilute it by chasing ghosts.
              Build the Luciano case. That's what
              matters. That's what your brother
              would have wanted.

The mention of his brother silences Brennan. Hartley knows exactly
which button to press.

                         BRENNAN
              Yes, sir.

                         HARTLEY
              Good man. Keep me posted.

The line goes dead. Brennan holds the receiver for a moment. Sets it
down.

He looks at his corkboard. The web of names and connections. He picks
up a photograph of Genovese. Studies it. Then puts it facedown on
the desk.

EXT. HAVANA - WATERFRONT - DUSK

Brennan and Delgado walk along the Malecon seawall. The sun is
setting. Fishermen pull their boats ashore. The city glows amber.

                         DELGADO
              What did your director say?

                         BRENNAN
              Stay focused on Luciano.

                         DELGADO
              And Genovese?

                         BRENNAN
              Not my assignment.

Delgado nods slowly. Not surprised.

                         DELGADO
              So we keep going.

                         BRENNAN
              We keep going.

They walk. The waves break against the seawall. The sun drops lower.

                         DELGADO
              My daughter asked me last night what
              I do at work. My little one.
              Ana. She's five.

                         BRENNAN
              What did you tell her?

                         DELGADO
              I told her I keep people safe.

A beat.

                         DELGADO (CONT'D)
              She asked which people.

Brennan looks at him. Delgado looks at the water.

                         DELGADO (CONT'D)
              I didn't have an answer.

They walk on in silence. The sun sets. The lights of Havana come on
one by one, and the two men disappear into the city, walking the same
direction but carrying very different doubts.

## Production Notes
- The phone calls to Hartley are critical exposition scenes -- they need to convey information while maintaining tension
and planting dramatic irony
- Brennan's investigation should feel methodical and real: notes, photographs, maps of the heroin routes, names connected
by lines
- The intelligence anomaly is a subtle but important plant -- Brennan notices something wrong and chooses to ignore it.
This choice must feel natural, not stupid
- Delgado's "bigger monster" argument should land with weight -- the audience should feel the moral complexity, not a
simple good-vs-evil framework
- January in Havana: the holiday atmosphere is gone, replaced by the workaday reality of an ongoing operation
- Two Hartley phone calls should feel different in tone: the first is warm and validating, the second is sharp and
deflective
