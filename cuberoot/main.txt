# Cube^(0.333)
Alternate Title: Cube Root

## Logline
When a corporate food science team is forcibly reassigned to a dangerous dimensional physics division, they discover their company's experiments have created a fractional-dimension cube that consumes everything around it — and they're already trapped inside.

## Synopsis
Alex, a food scientist at the mega-corporation INZO, is reassigned from her successful sof-tak project to the Quantum Logistics division, where experiments with fractional dimensions have gone catastrophically wrong. Two employees — Barry and Josh — are already trapped inside an n=0.333 dimensional space that nobody can extract people from. A desperate message scratched into a piece of food sent through the transmitter reads: "WE'RE ALL DYING."

As the combined team scrambles to understand the crisis, physicist Nina reveals the root cube is acting like a singularity, actively consuming other dimensional spaces. The threat isn't contained — it's growing. Edgar, the oblivious corporate VP, finally grasps the stakes and directs the team to prepare an expedition kit. But when Edgar steps outside to grab dinner, he discovers the impossible: the building is already inside the root cube. They aren't sending an expedition. They ARE the expedition.

Trapped in the cube's n=3 rooms — identical spaces that mechanically rearrange to cause disorientation — the team navigates lethal traps, losing members one by one. Rashid dies solving a mathematical trap. Sofia is killed by a vacuum door when she follows Marcus's order to open what she thinks is an exit. The team fractures: Darius, distrusting corporate leadership, splits off with Morris, Nina, and Yuki. Chen dies defending Edgar from Darius's attack. Edgar is killed by a magnetic trap that targets his metal hip replacements. The survivors split further — Marcus goes alone to build a technical escape, while Alex leads a small group to keep exploring with Josh as guide.

In the final act, Darius turns on Morris and kills him, only to be killed by Nina and Yuki in retaliation. The surviving groups converge at the lab building, where Nina and Yuki have secretly solved the memory erasure problem — the key obstacle to escaping the cube. They must decide whether to escape knowing the cube is still active and consuming people, or stay and try to stop it.

## Theme
Corporate profit versus human life — and the cost of experimentation when the experimenters become the experiment.

The story asks what happens when the people who built the cage end up inside it, and whether the institutional thinking that created the disaster can also solve it.

## Genre
Sci-Fi / Horror / Thriller

## Setting
**Near-future corporate research campus — INZO Corporation**

A sprawling corporate facility housing multiple divisions, from mundane food science to classified dimensional physics research. The story moves between corporate normalcy and dimensional nightmare.

**Key Locations:**
- **INZO Food Science Lab** - Alex's home turf, where sof-tak (molecular compression food) is developed. Normal, friendly workplace.
- **Quantum Logistics Building** - A separate, more secure cube-like structure housing dimensional experiments. Sterile, high-tech, ominous.
- **Edgar's Office / Hallways** - Corporate spaces where profit-driven decisions are made far from the science.
- **The Root Cube (n=0.333 space)** - Fractional-dimension environment containing n=3 cube rooms that mechanically rearrange. Identical rooms, ladder rungs around centered doors, fractal-tile walls. Lethal traps. Infinite scope — cubes within cubes within cubes.
- **The Lab Building (inside the cube)** - The QL building, now dollhouse-sized inside the root cube, serving as the team's base and equipment cache.

## MAIN CHARACTERS

### Alex
Food scientist at INZO, protagonist. Reassigned from Food Experimentation to Quantum Logistics against her will. Practical, principled, willing to challenge authority. Emerges as the group's moral leader inside the cube. Advocates for exploration and learning over brute-force engineering. Her sof-tak molecular compression technology becomes critical for survival (shrinking food supplies).

### Marcus
Head of Quantum Logistics. Carries the weight of knowing the real dangers while managing corporate expectations. Pragmatic and guilt-ridden — Sofia's death under his command haunts him. Inside the cube, his leadership style shifts from collaborative to isolated. Splits from the group to pursue a technical escape solution alone, taking communication equipment to try to engineer a way out. Injured (ankle burn) and increasingly resentful of the deaths he feels responsible for.

### Lea
Dimensional physicist on the Quantum Logistics team. Alex's college friend — their reunion provides emotional grounding. Carries a personal burden: Josh, who she had unspoken romantic feelings for, is one of the trapped employees. Her technical expertise in dimensional physics is essential for understanding the cube's mechanics. Torn between the competing approaches of Marcus and Alex, she ultimately joins Alex's exploratory group.

### Edgar
VP of Logistics. Initially the stereotypical oblivious corporate executive obsessed with "dollarinos" and "synergy." Doesn't understand the science and doesn't care to — until Nina's revelation that the cube is consuming everything. His transformation from corporate fool to committed leader is one of the story's strongest arcs. Volunteers to stand closest to danger, organizes rationing when trapped, unites the team. Dies in Act 2 when a magnetic trap targets his metal hip replacements — his heroic arc cut tragically short.

### Josh
Theoretical physicist who was trapped in the root cube for weeks before the team arrives. Changed by the experience — speaks in cryptic warnings ("exits lie," "keep moving," "no out, only through"). Becomes the team's guide through the cube. More lucid when discussing science. Survived by learning the cube's patterns; his colleague Barry did not. Carries Barry's research notebook containing critical navigation data.

### Darius
Food scientist, antagonist. Intelligent and perceptive — catches critical details others miss. Distrusts corporate leadership (Marcus, Edgar, Lea) and believes they'll sacrifice non-managers to survive. Splits from the team after Rashid's death, calling them "bootlickers." His prediction that following leadership means death proves partially right (Sofia, Chen, Edgar all die). But his own path leads to violence — he kills Morris and is killed by Nina and Yuki.

### Morris
Food scientist, comic relief. Absent-minded, prone to rambling, but genuinely brilliant. His scientific enthusiasm provides levity ("terrifying and people are trapped and we might die, but scientifically? This is groundbreaking"). Defects to Darius's group out of fear after witnessing multiple deaths. Proposes the initial "mind shield" thought experiment that leads to solving memory erasure. Killed by Darius in Act 3.

### Sofia
Lab chemist on the Food Experimentation team. Supports the QL team's safety questions, empowering them to speak up. Has children at home — leaves to check on them and never returns (her purse is found in the lobby after the building is consumed). Found alive in the cube but killed when she opens a door to vacuum on Marcus's command. Her death validates Darius's distrust of leadership.

### Nina
Mathematician on the Quantum Logistics team. Her analysis reveals the root cube is a singularity consuming all dimensions — the discovery that changes everything. Defects to Darius's group in Act 2 out of disillusionment with Lea siding with company leaders. In Act 3, she and Yuki secretly solve the memory erasure problem and build protective devices without telling Marcus — the ultimate expression of independence from authority.

### Yuki
Mathematician on the Quantum Logistics team. Traumatized by Rashid's death and Sofia's death — screams "Darius was right!" Defects to Darius's group. Makes the breakthrough insight that an electron barrier can shield neural patterns during dimensional transition, solving memory erasure. Works with Nina to build the devices in secret.

### Chen
Electrical engineer on the Quantum Logistics team. Close colleague of Barry (the first trapped employee). His jaw tightens when Barry's fate is discussed — the loss is personal. Loyal to a fault: rushes to defend Edgar when Darius attacks, and dies from dissolve burns in the resulting fight. His death embodies Josh's warning that "the cube punishes loyalty."

### Rashid
Mathematician on the Quantum Logistics team. Dies in Act 2 attempting to solve a mathematical trap puzzle. First death inside the cube, establishing that expertise alone isn't enough to survive. His death traumatizes Yuki and begins the team's fracture.

### Barry (deceased before story begins)
Dimensional physicist and instrumentation specialist. Sucked into n=0.333 during an experiment three weeks before the story begins. Resourceful — jerryrigged his device to send one-way messages back through. Built a "quantameter" for navigating dimensional space. Died inside the cube; Josh was present. His research notebook, recovered by Josh, contains critical data about dimensional navigation.

## KEY STORY ELEMENTS

### Sof-Tak and Molecular Compression
Alex's food science project — a bread-meat hybrid created using molecular compression technology. Can be shrunk to microscopic size and expanded back. Originally developed to solve hunger, it became INZO's most profitable product. Inside the cube, the compressed sof-tak becomes critical survival rations. The shrinking/growing technology connects food science to dimensional physics.

### The Root Cube (n=0.333)
A fractional-dimension space that INZO created through quantum experiments. Objects can be stored and retrieved perfectly, but people cannot be extracted — they go in and can't come out. The root cube acts as a singularity, consuming other dimensional spaces regardless of their dimensionality (n=0, n=3, n=4). It needs to periodically add mass to maintain itself, like a hungry organism.

### The n=3 Cube Rooms
Inside the root cube, the team navigates n=3 cube rooms — a 3x3x3 grid of 27 identical rooms. Each room has six faces with centered doors and ladder rungs. The rooms mechanically rearrange when doors close, causing extreme disorientation. There are no dimensional effects in these rooms — just physical shuffling of identical spaces. Walls have fractal-like tiles that emit light.

### Memory Erasure
When people transition from n=0.333 back to n=3 space, their memories are erased. The vibrational energy damps during the transition, destroying encoded memory information. This is why previous cube victims woke up with no memory. Nina and Yuki's electron barrier solution — inspired by the lab building's protective field — can shield neural patterns during transition. The breakthrough happens through Morris's initial thought experiment, Nina's correction, and Yuki's insight.

### Dimensional Navigation
Barry discovered that safe movement through the root cube requires understanding "dimensional orbitals" — stable shells at specific distances from dimensional nuclei. Root-space has three nuclei with overlapping orbital shells. Navigation by physical direction (xyz) is meaningless; you must navigate by orbital position. Barry built a quantameter to detect safe routes but achieved only 60% accuracy. The lab building sits at an orbital shell overlap, explaining its stability.

### The Team Empowerment Dynamic
The Quantum Logistics team has been suppressed under Marcus's management. When the Food Experimentation team arrives and fearlessly challenges authority (Alex confronting Edgar, Darius demanding answers, Sofia backing Nina's safety questions), the QL team gains confidence to act independently. This builds to Nina and Yuki secretly building memory shield devices in Act 3 — the ultimate expression of independence from authority.

### INZO's Corporate Brutality
INZO doesn't just fire underperformers — failed project teams become test subjects. "Low performers on failed projects don't get severance packages. They get test cases." This revelation, delivered by Marcus to Darius, explains why no one can simply walk away. The threat hangs over everyone: make the project profitable or become the research.

## DRAMATIC THEMES

1. **Corporate Ethics vs. Human Life** - Profit-driven experimentation creates existential threats
2. **The Cage Builders Become the Caged** - The team that built dimensional prisons is now trapped in one
3. **Leadership Under Crisis** - Edgar's transformation from corporate fool to genuine leader, cut short by death
4. **Trust and Division** - The cube doesn't need to kill everyone directly; it turns survivors against each other
5. **Knowledge vs. Action** - Alex's exploratory patience vs. Marcus's engineering urgency — both valid, neither guaranteed
6. **Loyalty Punished** - Chen dies defending Edgar; following orders kills Sofia; the cube exploits human bonds
7. **Independence as Survival** - Nina and Yuki's secret work saves everyone precisely because they didn't trust authority
8. **The Cost of Being Right** - Darius's warnings prove correct, but his path leads to murder and his own death
9. **Institutional Complicity** - Everyone who works at INZO is complicit in what it does, whether they know it or not

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Visual Style:**
- Corporate sterility of INZO contrasts with the alien geometry of the root cube
- Identical cube rooms create claustrophobic disorientation — same walls, same doors, same light
- Fractal-tile walls emit ambient light — bright enough to see, never dark, never safe
- Traps emerge from walls with no warning; the environment is the enemy
- Film references: Cube (1997), Alien, Ex Machina (corporate horror), Annihilation (dimensional wrongness)

**Key Visuals:**
- Sof-tak shrinking to microscopic and explosively expanding back
- "WE'RE ALL DYING" scratched into food skin
- View through lobby windows: impossible cube geometry where the parking lot should be
- QL building dollhouse-sized inside the root cube
- Magnetic field lines revealing hundreds of cubes stretching into infinity
- Floor dissolution spreading like acid across tiles

**Special Considerations:**
- Cube rooms must feel identical but not boring — lighting color changes, trap variations
- Dimensional transitions need distinct visual language (normal space → root space)
- Deaths should be visceral and have consequences — injuries persist, exhaustion matters
- The cube is mechanical, not magical — rearrangement is physical, disorientation comes from sameness
