# LIAM

## BASIC INFORMATION
- **Full Name**: Unknown (uses stolen identity "Liam [SURNAME TBD]")
- **Age**: 29, turning 30
- **Occupation**: Bartender (using false identity)
- **Family**: Unknown/unstated

### THE STOLEN IDENTITY
The name "Liam" is not his real name. He stole another man's SSN and ID to get his bartender job at Mulligan's. This is
how he stays invisible—no trail back to whoever he really is.

**How it works:**
- Stole a real person's identity (SSN, ID) to pass the bar's employment check
- Gave the bar a fake address on his paperwork—the apartment Sarah finds
- The real "Liam" is an innocent man living elsewhere under his own identity

**How it unravels:**
- Sarah gets "Liam's" name from the bar after Hank's murder
- She calls police with his identity (but NOT the address—she wants to find him herself first)
- Police run the identity and find the REAL Liam—arrest him
- After Scene 10 fight, cops call Sarah: "We got him"
- Sarah knows immediately this isn't her stalker

**Why this matters:**
- Sarah can't correct the police—she has no proof, no real name for the stalker
- Running is pointless: if he uses false identities, he could be anyone, anywhere
- The stalker remains a ghost—untraceable, unknowable
- Reinforces his "invisible man" archetype: even his name is stolen

## ARCHETYPE
**The Predator** - Drives entire narrative through obsession with hunting "worthy prey." Secondary: The Invisible Man -
his mundane invisibility fuels his need to prove superiority through violence.

## CHARACTER FUNCTION
The antagonist. A serial killer who wants to "level up" by hunting worthy prey. Drives the entire narrative through his
obsession with challenge.

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION
- Messy bowl cut
- Lanky but muscular enough for the fights
- Think Samuel Blenkin or Matt Smith type

## PERSONALITY TRAITS
- Invisible to everyone in daily life
- Views himself as skilled/elite despite mundane existence
- Takes offense easily - interprets jokes as humiliation
- Meticulous planner (goes through mail, observes in person)
- Emotionally volatile when things go wrong
- Sees not getting caught as "just another challenge"
- Avoids victim mentality by reframing bad outcomes as "unlucky" - skill is turning luck around
- Self-aggrandizing (compares himself to Count Zaroff)

## RELATIONSHIPS
- Regular customers: invisible to them, resents them
- Hank Reeves: sees as "worthy prey" - formidable opponent
- Sarah Reeves: adversary, respects as another challenge
- The children: collateral, not targets
- Bobby: adversary, kills in basement (Scene 15)
- Girly: tases her, later kills her in brutal fight

## CHARACTER ARC
- **Beginning State (Act 1)**: Invisible bartender with grandiose self-image, has killed before but finds "unworthy"
victims unsatisfying. Seeks a real challenge to prove his self-proclaimed skill.
- **Inciting Incident**: Humiliation at the bar triggers his decision to hunt "worthy prey" - someone who can fight back.
- **Escalation (Act 2)**: Finds Hank, stalks him, launches attack. Wins the fight but barely - Hank nearly kills him
multiple times. Wounded, he escapes but faces an unexpected new opponent: Sarah.
- **Crisis Point (Act 3)**: Returns armored and prepared, but the family's traps wound him severely. Kills Eli but Sarah
nearly kills him. Both are caught in the final trap.
- **End State**: Dies in suicide-by-cop wearing Hank's blackbelt as a trophy. Got the challenge he wanted - and it
consumed him. A Negative Arc: his obsession with proving his "skill" leads to his destruction.

## DIALOGUE STYLE
- Voiceover throughout Act 1 - self-aggrandizing, views himself as skilled
- V.O. uses formal, pseudo-intellectual language ("unlocked chasms," "possess so much skill") that masks narcissistic
grandiosity as objective self-assessment
- Consistently reframes failures as external forces ("took a shit on me," "got screwed") - never accepts personal
responsibility
- Shifts abruptly from self-pitying to coldly calculating when humiliated
- V.O. tone is clinical and detached even when discussing grievances
- Does NOT directly explain his motivations - never says "I do this because..." or connects his violence to his
insecurities out loud. His delusion keeps him from that level of self-awareness.
- Frames murder casually as "a little game" - playful, almost bored tone about killing
- Uses dismissive labels for victims ("this chump," "regular asshat") - dehumanizing language
- Challenges victims like a sportsman giving them a chance: "I want to see what you've got." — frames murder as a fair
test of skill, not cruelty
- Literary pretension - references Zaroff, sees himself as cultured predator rather than common criminal
- Victim-blaming worldview: "They just have luck. Bad luck." - victims deserve it for lacking skill
- Minimal spoken dialogue - when he does speak, it's performative ("Just... learning."), playing a role to blend in
- Premature confidence crumbles fast: "I thought this would be a challenge" → "This wasn't supposed to be this hard" →
"Just... fucking... DIE." - fantasy vs. reality
- "Sportsman" compliments for worthy opponents: "Oh, you're good..." and "Good game." - genuine respect, almost grateful
for the challenge
- Taunting invitations to continue the game: "Catch me if you can." - treats Sarah's threat as exciting rather than
threatening
- Mockery of targets that backfires: "Some expert." "Some fortress." - contempt before he understands the threat
- Crude vulgarity when desperate: "Body armor, bitch!" - drops the cultured predator facade under pressure
- Superiority complex as comedy: "HOW DARE YOU BURN ME!" - genuinely indignant that prey would injure him, absurd given
the context
- Cruel psychological warfare: mocks "Unky Buh-by?" up the stairwell, knowing the children's silence confirms their hope
is dead
- Analyzes threats even mid-combat: "But the lock is store-bought. Amateur mistake." - maintains superiority complex
through technical critique

### Scene 20 - The Crawling Struggle

## KEY SCENES
- **Act 1, Scene 1: The Humiliation** - Liam bartends invisibly while his V.O. reveals self-aggrandizing worldview ("I
possess so much skill"). A customer's casual joke about poisoning drinks triggers his shift from self-pity to cold
calculation: "People who get all the luck don't realize they have no skill. So I need to show them."
- **Act 1, Scene 2: Bar Revenge** - Liam stalks and kills Glasses Guy, a look-alike from the bar. Gives him a chance:
"I want to see what you've got." Trophy box contains cracked glasses and a child's hair bow (second kill). Apartment
reveals tinkerer side. After reading "The Most Dangerous Game": "The only prey worth hunting... is prey that can hunt
you back."
- **Act 1, Scene 3: The Cabela's Encounter** - Hunts for worthy prey at Cabela's, dismissing unworthy targets. Spots
Hank and Bobby. Observes Hank's expertise, engages briefly: "Just... learning. I work at a bar." V.O. selects Hank:
"This is the one. He's not pretending." Follows him home to rural property: "The knife man, Reeves, lives out here.
Away from the city. Isolated. Perfect."
- **Act 1, Scene 5: The Surveillance (Montage)** - Two days of reconnaissance. Learns the family, routines, property layout.
Notes the Rottweiler: "Eighty pounds, maybe more. Noted." Notes Hank's hunting trophies: "So he does hunt. The liar."
Plans to catch Hank outside: "Time to hunt."
- **Act 1, Scene 6: Let's Do This** - Family leaves. Liam gears up (knife, rope, bear mace). Watches Hank arrive home
alone. Final V.O.: "He's good. Maybe even great. But I'm better... Let's do this." VOICEOVER ENDS.
- **Act 2, Scene 7: The Flat Tire** - First real engagement. Bear trap tire, arrows (second one hits Hank's thigh), nail
trap in handrail. Premature confidence: "Looks like I overthought it. I thought this would be a challenge." Flashbanged,
thrown over porch railing. Genuine surprise: "Oh, you're good..." Hurls objects through windows: "We're just getting
started!"
- **Act 2, Scene 8: The Battle Inside** - Brutal indoor fight. Disarmed by Hank's sai, forced to use furniture and dirty
tricks. "This wasn't supposed to be this hard." Hank nearly kills him—spinning leg takedown, breaks Liam's ribs—but Liam
grabs a broken table leg and stabs him. "Good game... You were [the challenge]." Executes Hank with his own gun.
Headlights arrive—family returning.
- **Act 2, Scene 9: The Hostage** - Takes Olivia hostage to escape, blood still crusted on his face from Hank.
Calm, transactional: "I'm walking out of here. She comes with me to the car." When Sarah threatens to come for him:
"Catch me if you can." Smiles—he's found his next challenge. Maces the room and flees into the trees.
- **Act 2, Scene 13: The Apartment Fight** - Seen objectively for first time—pathetic apartment, murder wall. Bumps into
Sarah/Eli leaving. Brutal fight: nearly kills Sarah twice (knife, strangling) but Eli saves her both
times. Baton to skull dazes him. Escapes via fire escape but Sarah JUMPS and lands on him. Ground tussle—both too hurt to
finish it. Escapes before Eli can get down. Writes "CYA TONIGHT" on car window—Sarah wipes it off before police can see.
- **Act 2, Scene 14: The False Identity** - Cops call Sarah saying they arrested "Liam"—but it's the real identity theft
victim. His stolen identity gambit pays off: he remains a ghost.
- **Act 3, Scene 15: Parallel Preparation (MONTAGE)** - Heals in abandoned building. Keeps Hank's blackbelt as trophy:
"You earned this." Finishes armed drone (small gun attached), makeshift fish-scale armor, dirtbike helmet with metal plates. "Let's
finish this." Approaches the house at night.
- **Act 3, Scene 16: The Return** - Returns armored. Tases Girly: "Good dog." Battering rams door down. Triggers sealed
doors, flashbang, blind gunfire (armor saves him): "Body armor, bitch!" Molotov trap burns his leg. Fights Sarah on
stairs—wounded but presses. Hot oil trap burns him further: "HOW DARE YOU BURN ME!" Eli traps him inside: "Two men enter,
one man leaves." Retreats to basement.
- **Act 3, Scene 17: Bobby's Return** - Hears kids cheer "Unky Buh-by"—notes reinforcements. Sets accelerant trap on
basement stairs: "Should've stayed home, gun man." Bobby burns, dies with arm outstretched. "Spray and pray. Neither one
helped." Girly attacks—brutal fight shreds his armor. Gets Bobby's shotgun, shoots dog twice. Second shot flings Girly
into Bobby's arm: "Like he caught her." Mocks from stairs: "Unky Buh-by?" Silence confirms Bobby's death.
- **Act 3, Scene 18: Eli's Death** - Eli opens gatehouse doors seeking revenge: "The one who wouldn't let me leave."
Eli's arrow deflects off chest armor. Fight on landing—Eli wounds him with knife to side. Overpowers Eli with shotgun
blast to thigh. Eli's dying words hit something: "You aren't a man." Kills him viciously with knife: "Not even worth the
effort" (but voice is tight—it stung). Races Sarah for open doors, blocks her. Sarah maces him, retreats. Kicks down
bedroom door, shoots off attic hatch lock: "But the lock is store-bought. Amateur mistake."
- **Act 3, Scene 19: The Escape** - Girls wait in secret room. Liam about to throw Sarah down passage, she yells for
girls to run. Girls open secret door, scream at scene—distracts Liam. Sarah uses momentum to push both down shaft (3
stories). Sarah dies from fall (Liam's grip on her neck). Liam very hurt.
- **Act 3, Scene 20: The Crawling Struggle** - Girls escape via step ladder from secret room. Liam removes last of armor,
walks out. Police arrive. Ties Hank's blackbelt around head, does suicide-by-cop: "BANG BANG." Shot dead.
