# Scene 10 - Police Confrontation

## LOCATION
REEVES HOUSE - NIGHT (Same night as Hank's murder)

## CHARACTERS
Sarah, Eli, Emma, Olivia, Detective Martinez, Detective Cole, Crime Scene Techs

## SCENE LAYOUT

### Opening
Same night as Hank's murder. Police are still at the house, processing the scene. Hank's body hasn't been moved yet.

### Key Beats

1. Detectives question Sarah while crime scene techs work around them
2. Police ask about the guns—multiple shots fired, they're seizing ALL firearms as evidence (standard procedure)
3. Sarah pushes back: "He said he's coming back. That's a credible threat."
4. Police are skeptical—they'll look into it, but she should let them handle it
5. Sarah's work history comes out—she's a government lawyer, she's seen this before
6. The cops don't take her seriously. One mutters something about "letting us do our job."
7. Sarah watches them take Hank's body. Cold fury building.
8. FLASHBACK: The past case—Sarah had a domestic violence case, warned cops repeatedly, confronted the husband with
police present. He killed the wife anyway. The system failed.
9. FLASHBACK: Meeting Hank—Sarah starts self-defense classes after the case. Meets Hank on the mat. Immediate attraction.
10. FLASH FORWARD (IMAGINED): After the flashbacks, Sarah imagines a future where she's sitting across from someone,
older and hollow, saying "They never found who killed Hank." The flashbacks showed her the past—the system failing,
Hank saving her. The flash forward shows her the future if she lets it happen again. The imagined failure drives her
to act.

### Character Moments
- **Sarah**: Her legal background makes her distrust of police specific and personal—not paranoia, experience. The moment
she watches Hank's body leave, something hardens in her. The flashbacks reveal WHY: she tried it "the right way" once.
Someone died anyway.

### Closing
The flash forward shows what Sarah refuses to become—a hollow woman who trusted the system and lost. The flashbacks
reveal why she knows the system will fail: the past case, the body bag, history repeating. And in the self-defense class,
she met Hank—the good that's now gone. Back in the present, she stares at the door where Hank's body was wheeled out.
She's done waiting for the system.

## FULL SCRIPT

INT. REEVES HOUSE - FOYER - NIGHT

Crime scene tape. Evidence markers. TECHS photographing the destroyed living room.

Sarah sits on the stairs, wrapped in a shock blanket. Her eyes are red but dry now. Watching everything.

Two DETECTIVES stand over her. DETECTIVE MARTINEZ, sympathetic. DETECTIVE COLE, impatient.

                    DETECTIVE MARTINEZ
          Mrs. Reeves, I know this is difficult. But we need
          to understand what happened.

                    SARAH
          I told you what happened. He killed my husband.
          He took my daughter hostage. I shot at him. He ran.

                    DETECTIVE COLE
          Multiple firearms discharged. We're going to need
          to take all of them. Standard procedure.

Sarah's head snaps up.

                    SARAH
          All of them?

                    DETECTIVE COLE
          Ballistics. We need to determine
          which weapon killed your husband.

                    SARAH
          I know which weapon killed my husband. The knife
          in that man's hand.

A TECH approaches holding a small .22 RIFLE.

                    TECH
          Detective—what about this one? It's a .22.

Cole glances at it. Shakes his head.

                    DETECTIVE COLE
          That's not involved. We're looking at the handguns
          that were discharged tonight. Leave it.

The Tech sets the .22 back against the wall.

                    DETECTIVE MARTINEZ
          Mrs. Reeves—

                    SARAH
          He said he's coming back. Those were his words.
          "Catch me if you can." That's a credible, specific
          threat of future violence.

Detective Cole exchanges a look with Martinez.

                    DETECTIVE COLE
          We'll look into it. But right now, we need to
          process the scene and let you—

                    SARAH
          I'm a government lawyer, Detective.

She stands. The blanket falls away.

                    SARAH (CONT'D)
          I know exactly what happens next. You'll investigate
          for a few weeks. Hit a dead end. The case goes cold.
          Meanwhile, he's out there. Planning.

                    DETECTIVE COLE
          Ma'am, if you'd let us do our job—

                    SARAH
          I've seen your job. I've seen murderers walk because
          evidence got lost. Because witnesses recanted. Because
          some DA decided it wasn't worth the trouble.

She steps closer to Cole.

                    SARAH (CONT'D)
          You're going to take my guns, tell me to sit tight,
          and leave me here with three children and a man who
          promised to come back.

                    DETECTIVE MARTINEZ
          We can arrange increased patrols—

                    SARAH
          Patrols.

She laughs. No humor in it.

A TECH approaches, interrupts.

                    TECH
          Detectives. Coroner's ready to move the body.

Sarah goes still.

Through the doorway, she see Hank's hand with wedding band poking from the black bag. Then zipped closed.

Emma, Olivia, and Eli huddle in the kitchen doorway, watching too.

Sarah's jaw tightens. Something hardens behind her eyes.

FLASHBACK — INT. GOVERNMENT OFFICE — DAY (YEARS EARLIER)

A younger SARAH. Late twenties. Professional suit. Stacking case files at her desk. A COLLEAGUE drops a folder in front
of her.

                    COLLEAGUE
          Henderson case. DA reduced the charges.
          He's out on bail.

Sarah goes still.

                    YOUNGER SARAH
          I told them. I told them he can't be allowed to go near her.

                    COLLEAGUE
          She called again. She's terrified—

Sarah grabs her jacket. Already moving.

FLASHBACK — EXT. SUBURBAN HOUSE — NIGHT (SAME ERA)

Red and blue lights. An AMBULANCE. Sarah stands behind police tape, staring at a BODY BAG being wheeled out.

A UNIFORMED COP approaches.

                    COP
          You the lawyer? We had officers on scene
          when it happened. He got past them.

Sarah stares at the body bag. Same image she just watched with Hank.

                    COP (CONT'D)
          Ma'am, there was nothing you could have—

                    YOUNGER SARAH
          Don't.

She walks away. The cop calls after her. She doesn't turn around.

FLASHBACK — INT. COMMUNITY CENTER — NIGHT (WEEKS LATER)

A self-defense class. Sarah, still wound tight, punches a sparring partner too hard then kicks their legs out so they go onto the mat. The INSTRUCTOR
steps forward.

                    INSTRUCTOR
          Whoa—easy. This is a beginner's class.

Sarah doesn't ease up. She's looking for the next person to hit.

Hank steps onto the mat. No scar on his face yet. Younger. Grinning.

                    HANK
          Mind if I try?

Sarah squares up. They spar. He's strong—absorbs her hits without flinching. But he doesn't hit back hard. Just
redirects. Calm.

She tries harder. She throws herself off balance. Hank uses this to flip her to throw her to the mat. Sarah braces for the impact, but Hank catches her before she hits it.

                    HANK (CONT'D)
          You're good.

                    YOUNGER SARAH
          You're annoying.

                    HANK
          I get that a lot.

They hold eye contact. First time Sarah's almost smiled in weeks.

END FLASHBACKS.

INT. REEVES HOUSE — FOYER — PRESENT

Sarah stands alone in the foyer. The police cars pull away outside. The house is quiet.

She stares at the door where Hank's body was wheeled out.

The memory of meeting Hank—his grin, his calm, the way he absorbed her darkness and gave back warmth—dissolves into
something else. Something that hasn't happened yet.

FLASH FORWARD (IMAGINED) — INT. UNKNOWN ROOM — DAY (YEARS FROM NOW)

An older SARAH. Hollowed out. Gray at the temples. Sitting across from SOMEONE we can't quite see. A friend, a
therapist, a reporter—it doesn't matter. What matters is her voice.

                    OLDER SARAH
          They never found who killed Hank.

She stares at nothing.

                    OLDER SARAH (CONT'D)
          Police said they'd look into it.
          Case went cold. They moved on.
          I never did.

The image of this future—this hollow, defeated woman who trusted the system and lost—burns into Sarah's mind.

END FLASH FORWARD.

INT. REEVES HOUSE — FOYER — PRESENT

Sarah's face has changed. Whatever softness was left is gone. The flashbacks showed her the past—the system failing,
Hank saving her. The flash forward showed her the future if she lets it happen again.

Sarah, barely a whisper.

                    SARAH
          Not again.

## Production Notes

**Timing:**
- Same night as Hank's murder
- Police processing the crime scene
- Hank's body removed at end of scene

**Key Story Elements:**
- Guns seized as evidence establishes why family can't just "shoot him" in Act 3
- Sarah's legal background (government lawyer) makes her distrust specific, not paranoid
- "Catch me if you can" callback from Scene 9
- The body bag moment is the turning point—Sarah's last restraint dies with Hank
- FLASH FORWARD (IMAGINED): After the flashbacks, Sarah imagines a future where she's telling someone "They never
  found who killed Hank." The flashbacks showed her the past (system failed, Hank saved her). The flash forward shows
  the future if she lets it happen again. This imagined failure is what breaks her—she refuses to become that hollow
  woman.
- FLASHBACK: Past case parallels current situation—she warned cops, they failed, someone died. Establishes why she won't
trust the system again
- FLASHBACK: Meeting Hank at self-defense class—she started fighting BECAUSE the system failed. Hank was the first person
who could match her intensity
- The two body bags (past case + Hank) create visual parallel—history repeating
