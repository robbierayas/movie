# Scene 1 - The Humiliation
----------------------------------------

INT. BAR - NIGHT (FLASHBACK)

A generic sports bar. TVs flicker. LIAM (29) wipes down the counter with mechanical precision. Customers talk around him,
through him, never to him.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          I'm the most skilled guy I know. I've just always been unlucky.

A REGULAR CUSTOMER slides onto a stool. Unheard due to V.O., Liam gets the customer's order and makes the drink. Customer
keeps talking to Liam.

                    REGULAR CUSTOMER
          This guy at work, I swear to God—

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Every time things started going good, something took a shit on me. Got good grades in high school, just to get
          screwed
          out of the best school.

Liam finishes making the drink and brings it over. He sits back against back bar listening to customer continue.

                    REGULAR CUSTOMER
          Sometimes I wish I could just...

Unheard due to V.O., customer continues gesturing beating up coworker, laughing with Liam.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          The college I went to? They just tried to force me in a cookiecutter. Then I am back at home and, soon as I
          made a little
          money, my mom kicked me out.

                    LIAM
          You know what you could do?

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          But I'm lucky too. Lucky I possess so much skill to overcome the bad luck. When I got shut out, I put my mind
          to it.
          Unlocked chasms I didn't even know were there. Then I had the skill to fill them with everything I needed.

The Regular Customer looks interested and listens to whatever Liam says. Nods. Almost smiles, but looks uncomfortable
now.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Now, I've gotten to a good point. Figured out how to make my way around without too much trouble. And all my
          skill is
          actually paying off.

The bar door opens. THREE FRIENDS enter—two men and an ATTRACTIVE WOMAN. They spot the Regular Customer and descend on
him with the easy confidence of people who've never been invisible.

FRIEND #1
There he is!

                    ATTRACTIVE WOMAN
          What are we drinking?

They crowd the bar. The Regular Customer gestures toward Liam.

                    REGULAR CUSTOMER
          Whatever you can handle, m'dear.

FRIEND #2
What's good here?

Liam opens his mouth to answer—

Regular customer, mock whisper, loud enough for everyone.

                    REGULAR CUSTOMER
          Actually, maybe order from the other bartender...

He leans toward his friends conspiratorially.

                    REGULAR CUSTOMER
          ...in case this guy puts something in the drink.

Laughter.
Liam stands frozen. Unsure what to do. The Attractive Woman covers her mouth. Friend #1 claps the Regular Customer on the
shoulder. They migrate to the other end of the bar, still laughing. Liam pretends to be looking the other direction and
looking for his rag.

Liam (v.o.), cold.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          People who get all the luck don't realize they have no skill. So I need to show me.

His eyes track the group in a mirror. The Regular Customer is already telling another story, Liam forgotten completely.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          But if I want to get anywhere... get what I deserve... I'm going to have to press my luck again and use my
          skills.

Liam's expression hasn't changed. But something behind his eyes has shifted. Calculated. Patient.

He picks up the rag and resumes wiping the counter.


----------------------------------------
# Scene 2 - Bar Revenge
----------------------------------------

EXT. DOWNTOWN STREET - NIGHT (FLASHBACK CONTINUES)

City lights reflect off wet pavement. LIAM walks with purpose, hands in his jacket pockets. Twenty feet ahead, a group of
FOUR PEOPLE laugh and stumble together. Among them—a MAN WITH GLASSES who bears a passing resemblance to the Regular
Customer.

Liam doesn't hide. He walks like any other pedestrian.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          So we play a little game. Obviously, I would like to test that regular asshat, but this will have to do. The
          most
          important part of my game is not getting caught. So I'll teach this chump.

The Man With Glasses says something that makes the group laugh. He adjusts his glasses—a nervous habit.

The group pauses at a crosswalk. Liam stops too, maintaining distance. The Man With Glasses glances back—just a casual
look, nothing suspicious.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          I have to make a stand. Put people in their place. But I'm not going to boss anyone around. That would make me
          just as
          unskilled as them.

The light changes. The group crosses.

EXT. SIDE STREET - CONTINUOUS

The group reaches a corner. Hugs. Goodbyes. The Man With Glasses waves them off and turns down a quieter side street,
alone now, hands in his coat pockets.

Liam rounds the corner. The streetlights here are spaced further apart. Pools of darkness between them.

The Man With Glasses walks faster. Not running. Just... aware.

He glances over his shoulder. The street behind him is empty.

He relaxes slightly. Keeps walking.

Liam emerges from a doorway alcove thirty feet ahead. The Man With Glasses doesn't see him yet—he's looking at his phone.

The Man With Glasses looks up from his phone. Sees Liam walking toward him. Their eyes meet.

Something in Liam's expression makes the man's step falter.

Man with glasses laughs.

                    MAN WITH GLASSES
          Hey, can I help you with something?

Liam doesn't answer. Keeps walking.

                    MAN WITH GLASSES
          Look, I don't want any trouble—

He backs toward an alley entrance. Glances into the darkness—empty. When he looks back, Liam is closer.

                    MAN WITH GLASSES
          I've got money, okay? Is that what you want?

                    LIAM
          Does everyone think skill can be bought?

Liam's fist connects with his jaw. The Man With Glasses goes down hard, glasses skittering across the pavement.

EXT. ALLEY - CONTINUOUS

Liam drags him into the shadows. The Man With Glasses scrambles backward, blood on his lip.

                    MAN WITH GLASSES
          Take it! Take whatever you want!

He fumbles out his wallet, throws it at Liam's feet.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          They always say that. Like skill is something you can buy.

Liam kicks the wallet aside. Spreads his arms slightly. Waiting.

                    MAN WITH GLASSES
          Please—

The Man With Glasses lunges—not at Liam, but for his bag on the ground. He pulls out a small canister.

MACE.

He sprays wildly. The cloud catches them both. Liam staggers back, eyes burning. The Man With Glasses swings
blindly—connects with Liam's temple. Then his ribs. Then his jaw.

Liam takes the blows. Tears streaming, eyes swelling shut. But he doesn't retreat.

The Man With Glasses winds up for another punch—

Liam catches his wrist. Twists. CRACK.

The man screams. Liam's other hand finds his throat.

The struggle is brief. Desperate. Then still.

Liam kneels there, breathing hard, eyes burning and swollen. He blinks through tears at the body beneath him.

He picks up the man's glasses. Holds them up to the distant streetlight. One lens is cracked.  He pockets the glasses.
Grabs the messenger bag and rifles through it—takes nothing useful, just slings it over his shoulder as he walks deeper
into the alley.

INT. LIAM'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

A small studio. Neat. Impersonal. The only decoration: a wooden box on a shelf.

Liam enters, face swollen, eyes red and watering.

He goes to the shelf. Opens the wooden box with care.

Inside: he places the cracked glasses gently beside—

A HAIR BOW. Pink. Faded. Small enough to belong to a child.

Liam stares at it for a moment. Then closes the box.

He moves to his small table. He dumps the messenger bag on the table. A dog-eared paperback pops out: "THE MOST DANGEROUS
GAME" by Richard Connell.

Liam sits. Rubs his aching eyes. Opens the book to a page near the end.

He reads silently. We see his swollen eyes moving across the text.

He sets the book down. Stares at nothing.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          I finished the book. And I realized I'd been wasting my time. These people—they don't have skill. They just
          have luck.
          Bad luck.

He glances at the wooden box on the shelf.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          I need more, a real challenge. Someone with actual skill. Someone dangerous. Someone who can fight back. Zaroff
          understood. The only prey worth hunting... is prey that can hunt you back.

Liam closes the book. A small smile forms on his swollen, bruised face.

He looks out his small window at the city lights.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Time to find a real challenge.

# Scene 3 - The Cabela's Encounter
----------------------------------------

INT. CABELA'S - DAY

The massive outdoor retailer sprawls before us. Taxidermied animals mounted on walls. Rows of camping gear, fishing
equipment, firearms behind glass. A hunter's paradise.

Liam enters, hands in pockets. He looks like every other weekend warrior browsing—unremarkable. Invisible.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          After Zaroff, I knew what I needed. Someone with skill. Real skill. Someone who could actually fight back.

He drifts through the aisles, eyes scanning customers. A HEAVYSET MAN examines a tent, struggling with the display model.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Not him. Couldn't outrun a head start.

A YOUNG GUY in tactical gear poses for a selfie with a crossbow.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          All the gear, none of the skill. Probably never left the suburbs.

An ELDERLY COUPLE debates fishing rod prices. Liam giggles to himself looking at them.

Liam pauses near the knife display, pretending to examine a hunting blade. His eyes continue scanning.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          It seems like hunting for who I am gonna hunt is gonna be the biggest hunt of all. It's all right. Probably
          need to wait the heat to cool off anyway

LOUD LAUGHTER booms from across the store—the kind of laugh that makes strangers smile. Two men round the corner into the
camping section.

Hank REEVES (early 40s) has the build of a thinner, younger John Goodman—big frame, presence that fills the aisle. His
FRIEND BOBBY trails behind him—soft around the middle, patchy beard, camo jacket even though they're indoors.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Now that's interesting.

Hank moves through the store like he owns it. Not arrogant—comfortable. He knows where everything is.

                    HANK
          —and she's standing there, covered in mud, holding this tiny fish like it's a trophy marlin.

Bobby, cackling.

                    BOBBY
          No shit?

                    HANK
          Hand to God. Olivia goes, "Daddy, I caught dinner." Thing was maybe four inches.

                    BOBBY
          Please tell me you ate it.

                    HANK
          Hell yes we ate it. Breaded it, fried it up. She was so damn proud. Sarah had to cut it in pieces so everyone
          could have a bite.

Bobby shakes his head, grinning. Liam drifts closer, keeping displays between them.

                    BOBBY
          Man, your kids are something else. Mine just stare at their phones.

                    HANK
          You gotta get 'em outside, Bobby. Drag 'em if you have to.

                    BOBBY
          Yeah, yeah. Hey, you see the game last night?

                    HANK
          Didn't catch it. Had the girls helping me reinforce the chicken coop. That fox has been circling again.

                    BOBBY
          You and that damn fox. Just shoot it already.

                    HANK
          Where's the sport in that? Besides, I want the kids to figure out the problem. Eli rigged up a motion sensor
          last week. Thing goes off, lights up the whole yard.

Bobby laughs.

                    BOBBY
          Next you'll have 'em digging trenches.

                    HANK
          Already got a bunker.

Bobby stops walking.

                    BOBBY
          Wait, for real?

Hank, winking.

                    HANK
          Storm shelter. Tornado country, Bobby. You know that.

                    BOBBY
          Uh huh. "Storm shelter."

Bobby stops at a display of emergency rations, picks one up.

                    BOBBY
          How long do these things last anyway?

                    HANK
          Those? Twenty-five years if you store 'em right. Temperature controlled, low humidity.

He takes the package from Bobby, examines the seal with practiced eyes.

                    HANK
          This brand's decent. Not the best sodium content, but the calorie density is solid. I got a rotation system at
          home—first in, first out. Never eat anything older than ten years.

                    BOBBY
          Jesus, Hank. You expecting the apocalypse?

                    HANK
          I'm expecting nothing. That's the whole point.

He sets the package back, straightens the display without thinking about it.

                    HANK
          My grandfather told me something once. "The time to fix the roof is when the sun is shining."

                    BOBBY
          That's some fortune cookie shit right there.

Hank laughs.

                    HANK
          JFK said it, asshole.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Listen to him. He's not guessing. He knows.

They move toward the firearms section. Liam follows at a distance, pausing to examine a camping stove.

The FIREARMS COUNTER stretches across the back wall. Rifles, shotguns, handguns gleaming under fluorescent lights.
Bobby's eyes go wide.

                    BOBBY
          Oh hell yes. Now we're talking.

He practically jogs to the display, pointing at a tactical shotgun with an extended magazine.

                    BOBBY
          Look at this beautiful bastard. Pray and spray, baby. Pray and spray.

                    HANK
          That's nice.

                    BOBBY
          Nice? This thing could stop a bear.

                    HANK
          So could a .45. So could a crossbow. So could a sharpened stick if you know where to put it.

                    BOBBY
          Here we go.

                    HANK
          I'm just saying—

Bobby, mock announcer voice.

                    BOBBY
          Ladies and gentlemen, Mister Karate Man is about to tell us why his bare hands are better than firearms.

Hank laughs, that booming sound again.

                    HANK
          Not my bare hands. Knives.

                    BOBBY
          The hell is the difference?

                    HANK
          About eight inches of steel.

He moves toward the knife display. Bobby follows, still glancing back at the shotgun longingly.

                    HANK
          Look, guns are fine. I got three at home. But close quarters? Home invasion? Knife's better.

                    BOBBY
          Bullshit.

                    HANK
          Knife doesn't jam.

                    BOBBY
          Neither does a Glock.

                    HANK
          Knife doesn't run out of ammo.

                    BOBBY
          That's what extended mags are for.

                    HANK
          Knife doesn't make noise that tells everyone in a mile radius where you are and that you're fighting for your
          life.

Bobby opens his mouth. Closes it.

                    HANK
          And in tight spaces—hallway, doorway, around a corner—

He makes a quick, efficient gesture with his hand.

                    HANK
          —you can't miss. Gun, you gotta aim. Knife, you just move.

                    BOBBY
          Alright, alright. I still say you're crazy.

Hank picks up a hunting knife, tests the balance.

                    HANK
          Sarah thought so too. Then I taught her. Now she's better than me with a blade.

                    BOBBY
          No way.

                    HANK
          Way. Faster hands. Lower center of gravity. She'd gut you before you knew she was there.

Bobby laughs nervously.

                    BOBBY
          Remind me to never piss off your wife.

Hank spots Liam examining knives nearby. Their eyes meet.

Hank nods.

                    HANK
          Hey, my man over there gets it. Look at him—he's not just browsing. He's testing the weight, checking the edge.
          That's someone who takes it serious.

Liam looks up, gives a small awkward nod—playing the shy customer caught eavesdropping.

                    LIAM
          Sorry, couldn't help overhearing. You military?

                    HANK
          Nah. Just practical. You?

                    LIAM
          Just... learning. I work at a bar. Sometimes things get rough.

                    BOBBY
          What, like fights?

                    LIAM
          Sometimes. I figure a knife is good to have. If someone bigger overpowers me, at least the damage won't be too
          bad. Better than a bottle to the head.

Bobby nods, getting it.

                    BOBBY
          Smart. Which bar?

                    LIAM
          O'Malley's. Downtown.

                    BOBBY
          No shit? I've been there. Good wings.

Liam gives a thin smile—playing the role perfectly.

Hank nods approvingly, that warm presence radiating out.

                    HANK
          Best time to learn is before you need it. Most people think they'll figure it out when something happens. They
          won't. The time to prepare—

Bobby, muttering.

                    BOBBY
          —is when the sun is shining.

Hank, grinning.

                    HANK
          He's learning.

He claps Bobby on the shoulder hard enough to stagger him.

                    HANK
          Come on, I gotta grab some paracord before Sarah calls wondering where I am.

                    BOBBY
          You're so whipped.

                    HANK
          Happily, brother. Happily.

They head toward another aisle. Liam watches them go.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          This is the one. He's not pretending. He actually knows what he's doing.

Liam sets down the knife he was holding.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          The friend... gun guy. Spray and pray. That means panicking and hoping. The knife man though...

Hank picks up a bundle of paracord, tests its strength with a sharp tug. Even from across the store, the confidence in
his hands is obvious.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          He's the one I want.

EXT. CABELA'S PARKING LOT - DAY

Hank and Bobby exit, bags in hand. They walk toward a WEATHERED PICKUP TRUCK—practical, well-maintained, not flashy.

Bobby pulls out a cigarette, lights it.

                    BOBBY
          Alright, where to next?

                    HANK
          Grocery store. Gotta grab stuff for the cookout.

Bobby groans.

                    BOBBY
          Can't we just get burgers here? They got jerky.

                    HANK
          Jerky's not a cookout, Bobby.

Bobby takes a long drag, exhales.

                    BOBBY
          I don't know how you do it, man. The whole... family thing. You make it look easy.

Hank pauses, looks at his friend.

                    HANK
          It's not easy. It's just worth it.

He claps Bobby on the shoulder.

                    HANK
          Come on. One more stop and then we eat.

They toss their bags in the truck bed. Bobby climbs into the passenger side. Hank takes the wheel.

Thirty feet away, Liam exits the store. Hangs back near a row of cars. Watching.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Interesting. They came together.

Hank starts the pickup. Engine turns over.

Liam walks casually to his own car—a bland sedan, forgettable. He pulls out his phone, pretends to check something while
watching Hank's truck pull out of the space.

The license plate is clearly visible.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          There you are.

He types something into his phone. Then starts his car.

EXT. RURAL ROAD - DAY

Hank's truck drives down a two-lane highway, farmland stretching on either side. Trees lining the road.

Half a mile back, Liam's sedan follows. Patient. Unhurried.

The truck turns off onto a smaller road. A sign reads: PRIVATE PROPERTY - REEVES.

Liam slows. Doesn't follow. Instead, he pulls over to the shoulder.

Through the trees, he catches glimpses of the truck heading up a long driveway toward a distant house.

He pulls out his phone. Takes a photo of the road sign.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          The knife man lives out here. Isolated. Property. Family.

He puts the car in gear and drives past the driveway, continuing down the main road.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Perfect.

# Scene 4 - Family Time
----------------------------------------

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - DRIVEWAY - DAY

Hank's weathered pickup rolls up the long driveway. Bobby's in the passenger seat, grocery bags already in his lap.

Before they've even stopped, the front door bursts open.

Olivia sprints across the yard. EMMA follows. ELI walks behind them, trying to look cool.

A ROTTWEILER—GIRLY—bounds out, tail wagging furiously.

                    OLIVIA
          Daddy!

She crashes into Hank as he climbs out. He scoops her up.

                    HANK
          There's my girl!

Bobby climbs out the passenger side, grocery bags in hand. Girly immediately redirects, jumping at him.

                    BOBBY
          Hey! Down, you monster. Down!

He's laughing, not actually bothered. Girly ignores him, licking his face.

Sarah appears on the porch, wiping her hands on a towel.

                    SARAH
          You get everything?

                    HANK
          And then some. Bobby insisted on the "good" steaks.

Bobby, hauling bags.

                    BOBBY
          They were on sale!

                    SARAH
          Uh huh.

Emma takes some bags from Bobby. Eli grabs the cooler from Hank's truck bed.

                    EMMA
          Can we eat now? I'm starving.

Olivia, still in Hank's arms.

                    OLIVIA
          Wait! What about homeschool?

Everyone stops. The kids exchange looks.

Hank, sighing theatrically.

                    HANK
          She's right.

                    EMMA
          Dad, come on—

                    ELI
          We can do it after—

                    HANK
          Nope. Your sister's got a point. Homeschool first. Then we eat.

Groans all around. But no one really argues. This is routine.

Bobby turns to Sarah.

                    BOBBY
          Homeschool?

Sarah smiles.

                    SARAH
          You'll see.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - BACKYARD - DAY

A TRAINING DUMMY stands in the grass. Padded. Duct-taped. Well-used.

The family forms a loose circle. Hank stands in the center, rolling up his sleeves.

                    HANK
          Alright. Quick review. Eli, show me the disarm.

Eli steps forward. Hank grabs his wrist. In one smooth motion, Eli twists, breaks the grip, and has Hank's arm pinned
behind his back.

Hank, impressed.

                    HANK
          Good. Faster than last time.

                    ELI
          I've been practicing.

                    HANK
          I can tell. Emma, you're up.

Emma approaches nervously. Hank holds up his palms.

                    HANK
          Strike. Solar plexus.

Emma punches. Decent form, but tentative.

                    HANK
          Harder. You're not gonna hurt me.

She tries again. Better.

                    HANK
          Good. Now knee to the groin. Don't hold back.

Emma hesitates, glancing at Olivia.

                    EMMA
          Dad...

                    HANK
          On the dummy, Emma. On the dummy.

Emma, relieved.

                    EMMA
          Oh. Right.

She pivots to the dummy. Delivers a solid knee.

                    HANK
          There you go!

Bobby sits on the grass nearby, Girly sprawled across his lap. He's not really watching—he's roughhousing with the dog.

Bobby, baby-talking to Girly.

                    BOBBY
          Who's the best gorilla? Who's the best gorilla in the whole world?

Girly's tail wags furiously. She licks his face.

Olivia, correcting him.

                    OLIVIA
          Her name is Girly, Uncle Bobby!

                    BOBBY
          Then call me Unky Buh-by.

The kids burst out laughing. Even Emma cracks a smile.

                    OLIVIA
          Unky Buh-by!

                    BOBBY
          That's right. But look at her—she's a gorilla. Aren't you, girl?

Girly barks happily.

                    HANK
          Bobby. Get in here.

                    BOBBY
          What? No, I'm good—

                    HANK
          Come show the kids how not to do it.

Bobby groans but gets up. Girly follows, thinking this is a new game.

Bobby turns to the kids.

                    BOBBY
          Alright, watch and learn. This is what happens when you panic.

He throws a wild, exaggerated punch at Hank. Hank sidesteps easily.

Bobby stumbles forward, windmilling his arms.

                    BOBBY
          See? Panic.

The kids laugh. Even Eli cracks a smile.

Bobby throws another haymaker. Hank catches his wrist, spins him, and Bobby ends up face-down in the grass with Hank's
knee on his back.

Hank turns to the kids.

                    HANK
          What did he do wrong?

                    OLIVIA
          Everything!

More laughter. Bobby plays it up, tapping out dramatically.

                    BOBBY
          Okay, okay! I yield! Uncle!

Girly licks his face. Bobby sputters.

Sarah watches from the GRILL, spatula in hand. Smoke rises from sizzling burgers.

She's smiling. This is her family. This is her life.

It's good.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - BACKYARD - LATER

The family sits around a weathered PICNIC TABLE. Paper plates piled with burgers, corn, coleslaw.

Bobby's telling a story, gesturing with a burger in hand.

                    BOBBY
          —and he says to me, "Sir, that's not a parking spot." And I say, "It is now!"

Hank laughs, that booming sound. Sarah shakes her head. The kids are giggling.

Under the table, Girly moves between legs, hunting for dropped food. Olivia "accidentally" lets a piece of burger fall.

                    OLIVIA
          I made the travel team!

                    SARAH
          That's wonderful, honey!

                    EMMA
          Show-off.

                    OLIVIA
          You're just mad because you quit gymnastics.

                    EMMA
          I didn't quit. I... graduated.

Eli turns to Emma.

                    ELI
          Pass the ketchup.

                    EMMA
          Say please.

                    ELI
          Please, your highness.

Emma throws a chip at him. He catches it. Eats it.

                    HANK
          What about you, Eli? How's the archery going?

                    ELI
          Good. Coach says I might make varsity next year.

                    BOBBY
          Varsity? Damn, kid. I couldn't hit a barn door at your age.

Hank turns to Bobby.

                    HANK
          You bring Tyler next time. Kid needs to get out.

                    BOBBY
          Yeah, I keep saying that.

                    SARAH
          We'd love to have him. The more the merrier.

Hank puts his arm around Sarah. She leans into him.

The sun is setting behind the trees. Golden light on golden moment.

Everything is fine.

Everything is perfect.

CUT TO BLACK.

# Scene 5 - The Surveillance (MONTAGE)
----------------------------------------

MONTAGE - SURVEILLANCE (WEEKS PASS)

EXT. RURAL ROAD - NIGHT

Liam's sedan parked in the shadows, fifty yards from the Reeves driveway. He watches through binoculars. The house glows
warm in the distance.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          The knife man has a name. Hank Reeves.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - DAY

Liam walks up the driveway in an AMAZON VEST, package in hand. He lingers at the mailbox, rifling through envelopes.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Wife—Sarah. Three kids. Two girls, one boy.

He photographs the mail with his phone. Slides it back. Walks away.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          If I want Hank, I'll have to get him alone.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - DUSK

Liam crouches in the treeline with binoculars. Through the windows: TAXIDERMIED DEER HEADS on the walls. HUNTING PHOTOS.
COMPETITION RIBBONS.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          So he does hunt. The liar.

A beat.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Said he wasn't military. Just "practical." He's been hunting his whole life.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - VARIOUS TIMES

Quick cuts: SARAH's car pulls in, kids piling out with backpacks. A ROTTWEILER bounds to greet them—GIRLY. EMMA and
Olivia chase each other in the yard, the dog following. ELI loads archery gear into the truck. Sarah herds them back into
the car for practice.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Kids have activities. Wife drives them. House sits empty for hours.

He watches through binoculars as Girly patrols the yard.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          They've got a Rottweiler. Eighty pounds, maybe more. Noted.

Hank's truck pulls up the driveway. Alone. Work clothes dusty.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          But Hank comes home from work. Alone.

EXT. REEVES PROPERTY - DAY

Liam walks the perimeter at a distance, sketching in a small notebook. He notes windows, doors, the open ground
surrounding the house.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          Three floors—basement, main, upstairs. Long sightlines from the second story. No cover approaching the house.

He pauses. Studies the roofline.

                    LIAM (V.O.)
          He built this place like a fortress. Smart. But I'll come from outside. Catch him before he gets in.

INT. LIAM'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

Liam sits at his small table. PHOTOS spread out—the house, the family, Hank's truck. A hand-drawn SCHEDULE. A crude MAP
of the property.

He circles something on the schedule. Leans back.

                    LIAM (V.O.)

The blade finds a gap in Liam's damaged armor. Sinks into his side.

                    LIAM
          Fuck—

He HEADBUTTS Eli. The boy staggers back.

Liam rips the knife out of his own side. Blood flows.

He raises the shotgun. Aims at Eli's leg.

BOOM.

Eli's thigh EXPLODES. He screams. Crumples.

Can't stand. Can't run.

                    SARAH (O.S.)
          ELI!

                    LIAM
          Stay back!

Liam turns back to Eli. Draws his own KNIFE. The same one that killed Hank.

He crouches beside the wounded boy.

                    LIAM
          Brave kid. Stupid. But brave.

                    ELI
          You aren't a man.

Liam's expression flickers. The words hit something.

                    ELI
          You're just... a thing. A broken thing that pretends—

Liam drives the knife into Eli's chest. Vicious. Angry. The insult cut deep.

He TWISTS the blade. Once. Twice.

                    LIAM
          Not even worth the effort.

But his voice is tight. It stung.

Eli shudders. Goes still.

Sarah watches from above. Hand over her mouth. Tears streaming.

Her oldest child. Her son.

Dead.

Because he opened the doors.

                    SARAH
          No... no, no, no...

Liam stands. Wipes the blade on Eli's shirt.

Looks up at Sarah. Then at the OPEN GATEHOUSE DOORS behind her.

They both realize it at the same time.

The doors are open.

Sarah RUNS.

Liam CHARGES up the stairs.

INT. REEVES HOUSE - SECOND FLOOR HALLWAY - CONTINUOUS

Sarah sprints for the doors—

Liam is faster. He SLAMS through them before she can close them.

She pivots. Runs down the hallway.

Liam pursues.

Sarah ducks into a BEDROOM. Emma and Olivia are already there, retreating toward the closet with the attic ladder.

                    SARAH
          GO! UP! NOW!

She turns. Sees Liam entering the hallway.

Aims at the doorway—

UNLOADS.

A cloud of chemical fog fills the hallway. Liam walks straight into it.

                    LIAM
          FUCK—

He staggers. Eyes burning. Coughing. The Oakleys offer some protection but not enough.

Sarah SLAMS the bedroom door.

                    SARAH
          ATTIC! ALL OF YOU!

Emma helps Olivia up the ladder. Sarah follows.

THUD.

The door shudders. Liam on the other side.

THUD. THUD.

This door isn't reinforced. Regular bedroom door. Hollow core.

CRASH.

Liam's boot EXPLODES through the wood. He rips it off its hinges.

Eyes red and streaming. Rage.

The room is empty.

He sees the open closet. The attic ladder—pulled up.

Above, the ATTIC HATCH. Solid wood. Latched from inside.

Liam wipes his eyes. Studies the hatch.

                    LIAM
          Hank built this.

He examines the edges. The hinges. The latch.

                    LIAM
          Good work. Solid construction.

He raises the shotgun. Aims at the LOCK.

                    LIAM
          But the lock is store-bought. Amateur mistake.

BOOM.

The lock SHATTERS. Metal fragments spray.

He pushes up the hatch.

INT. REEVES HOUSE - ATTIC - CONTINUOUS

Dark. Cramped. Emergency supplies stacked against walls.

Sarah stands in the center. Knife in hand. Between Liam and her daughters.

Behind her, Emma pushes Olivia toward the SECRET PASSAGE entrance.

                    SARAH
          Go to the secret room. Wait there. Don't come out until I call.

                    EMMA
          Mom—

                    SARAH
          GO.

Emma takes Olivia's hand. They disappear into the passage.

Liam pulls himself through the hatch. Bloody. Burned. Maced. Stabbed.

But still coming.

                    LIAM
          Just us now.

Sarah raises the knife.

                    SARAH
          Just us.

                    LIAM
          No more running, Sarah.

                    LIAM
          No more running, Sarah.

Below her—three stories of vertical shaft. Darkness.

Liam enters the passage. Follows her down.

INT. SECRET BASEMENT ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Emma and Olivia huddle in the darkness. Surrounded by Hank's prepper supplies—canned food, water jugs, emergency gear.

                    OLIVIA
          Is that Mom?

                    EMMA
          Stay quiet.

INT. SECRET PASSAGE - MIDDLE LEVEL - CONTINUOUS

Liam is faster. Stronger. He gains on her.

Liam climbs over her. Pins her against the ladder.

                    LIAM
          Nowhere left to go.

                    LIAM
